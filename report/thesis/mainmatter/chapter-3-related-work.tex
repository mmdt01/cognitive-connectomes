\section{Networks of the brain}
\label{section:ch3_1}
\bigskip

Network neuroscience treats a nervous system as a graph and asks what its structure implies 
for its function. It supplies the framework within which the connectome-as-substrate question 
of this thesis can be posed at all, since asking whether biological connectivity confers 
computational advantage is, in the first instance, a graph-theoretic question about which 
features of the wiring carry weight. The field's apparatus, from the construction of networks 
out of empirical measurements to the analysis of degree, clustering, modularity, and hub 
structure, was consolidated as a programmatic discipline by Bassett and Sporns 
\cite{bassett2017network} and has matured into a self-conscious set of commitments 
that this thesis inherits.

The most consistent of those commitments is that the credibility of any structural claim 
depends on the quality of its comparison against an appropriate null model. Bassett and 
Sporns \cite{bassett2017network} treat this as a methodological aside; Bassett et al. 
\cite{bassett2018nature} elevate it to a validity criterion; Váša and Mišić 
\cite{vavsa2022null} make null modelling the entire subject of a review; and Seguin et al. 
\cite{seguin2023brain} extend the same standard to models of signal propagation. Within 
this lineage, Váša and Mišić \cite{vavsa2022null} articulate a graded spectrum of nulls 
running from liberal Erdős-Rényi randomisation through degree-preserving rewiring to 
spatially and cost-constrained variants, and argue that comparison against such a spectrum 
is how the contributions of distinct features are disentangled. This is the methodological 
idea the thesis's null-model ladder operationalises, and which the introduction to 
Chapter 1 named as the project's single binding commitment.
 
The case for a graded ladder rather than a single null follows from two related observations. 
The first is that what counts as non-random depends on the null chosen: Bassett and Sporns 
\cite{bassett2017network} treat small-worldness, modularity, and rich-club structure as 
robust regularities, but Váša and Mišić \cite{vavsa2022null} show that several dissolve under 
degree-preserving rewiring, and Seguin et al. \cite{seguin2023brain} note that 
small-worldness as canonically defined presupposes shortest-path signalling that is 
biologically implausible. The second is that features induce one another as byproducts: 
the joint presence of modules and hubs generates rich-club structure even when neither 
was the operative driver \cite{vavsa2022null}. Attributing an effect to a single feature 
therefore requires separating it from those it correlates with by construction, a caution 
anticipated more loosely by Bassett et al. \cite{bassett2018nature} and Suárez et al. 
\cite{suarez2020linking}.

A further consistent finding is that direct dyadic structure cannot, on its own, 
explain function. Suárez et al. \cite{suarez2020linking} and Seguin et al. 
\cite{seguin2023brain} both report that roughly half the variance in functional 
connectivity is unexplained by direct structural connectivity, because functional 
interactions reflect polysynaptic, higher-order processes that require an explicit 
model of signal propagation to recover. This is the field's most direct argument for 
treating the connectome as the substrate of a dynamical process rather than a static 
descriptor: a reservoir, in which input history is transformed by repeated propagation 
through fixed recurrent connections, is the kind of decentralised propagation model 
these reviews argue is necessary. Suárez et al. \cite{suarez2020linking} explicitly 
identify reservoir computing in this role, citing the connectome-reservoir work of 
Kawai et al. \cite{kawai2019small}, with the substantive treatment of that literature 
deferred to Section \ref{section:ch3_3}.

Running alongside this is a steady move toward annotated graphs in which microscale detail 
such as cytoarchitecture, neurotransmitter identity, and receptor expression augments the 
macroscale wiring \cite{bassett2017network, bassett2018nature, vavsa2022null, suarez2020linking}. 
This is the trajectory along which the planned progression up the biological-realism axis of 
Chapter 1 sits, with directedness, sign, and gap junctions among the annotations the field 
is endorsing.
 
Taken together, these reviews establish a graph-theoretic vocabulary for the connectome, 
an epistemic commitment to null comparison, a recognition that structure must be coupled 
to a propagation model to explain function, and a forward direction toward annotated graphs. 
What they do not substantively address is the use of a connectome as the fixed recurrent 
matrix of an artificial dynamical system in a controlled, null-laddered way: reservoir 
computing is mentioned with substance only once across the six reviews. Cell-resolution, 
single-organism connectomes such as that of C. elegans receive no substantive treatment, 
the reviews being macroscale-focused on tractography-derived human and macaque networks, 
and directed nulls preserving in- and out-degree sequences jointly, along with nulls 
preserving sign or excitatory-inhibitory balance, receive limited treatment. These 
omissions are taken up by the four sections that follow.




% Connectomics
\section{Searching for connectomes}
\label{section:ch3_2}
\bigskip

Where network neuroscience supplies the analytic apparatus, connectomics supplies the 
empirical substrate that apparatus operates on. Sporns et al. \cite{sporns2005human} 
introduced the term \emph{connectome} for a comprehensive, time-invariant description 
of the connections of a nervous system, conceived as a connection matrix whose entries 
can be successively annotated with direction, sign, and conduction delay. Empirical maps 
have since been produced at three nested scales: macroscale tract networks from diffusion 
imaging \cite{sporns2011human, sporns2013human}, single-cell reconstructions of invertebrate 
nervous systems \cite{cook2019whole, dorkenwald2024neuronal}, and a co-registered 
structural-functional volume of mouse visual cortex \cite{microns2025functional}. 
The thesis inherits both its current substrate and its candidate world-model substrate 
from this lineage.

A central tension organises the conceptual side of this literature. 
The Sporns programme treats the connectome as a foundational, time-invariant ground 
truth on which mechanistic accounts of brain function can be built 
\cite{sporns2011human, sporns2013human}. Bargmann and Marder \cite{bargmann2013connectome} 
push back: even the complete C. elegans connectome has been largely uninformative about 
which connections drive specific behaviours, because parallel pathways, neuromodulatory 
reconfiguration on a seconds-to-minutes timescale, and intrinsic neuronal dynamics together 
let the same anatomical wiring support many different functional outputs. Recent empirical 
work shifts the balance back. Vishwanathan et al. \cite{vishwanathan2024predicting} insert 
a synapse-resolved zebrafish brainstem connectome directly as the weight matrix of a linear 
rate model and statistically reproduce population-level coding properties measured in 
separate animals, and Lin and Murthy \cite{lin2025linking} reinforce this conclusion at 
cell-type resolution in the fly. The tension does not dissolve, since both works require 
some biological information beyond bare topology (a sign assignment by cell type in 
Vishwanathan, cell-type connectivity in the fly examples), but it shifts from whether 
structure can predict function at all to how much non-structural detail is the 
minimum needed. The thesis sits inside this updated tension, with the null-model 
ladder designed to map which features carry weight before biological enrichment is added.
 
A second pattern is the convergent retreat from treating nodes as identical, reached 
via parcellation at macroscale \cite{sporns2011human}, via cellular dynamics and modulation 
in invertebrates \cite{bargmann2013connectome}, and via an explicit annotated-connectome 
programme over gene transcription, receptor density, cell-type composition, and laminar 
architecture \cite{bazinet2023towards}. Vishwanathan et al. \cite{vishwanathan2024predicting} 
sharpen this into a methodological necessity: their model fails without an 
excitatory-inhibitory sign assignment by cell type. The thesis's biological-realism axis, 
climbing through directionality, sign under Dale's principle, gap junctions, and anatomical 
input-output routing, thus inherits a recognised programme rather than acting as an ad hoc 
sequence, with Section \ref{subsec: networks-of-the-brain}'s field-level move toward 
annotated graphs given specific computational consequence here.
 
A third convergence is methodological. Across organisms and scales, the connectome is 
increasingly inserted directly as the fixed weight matrix of a computational model whose 
dynamics are then asked to recover observed activity 
\cite{vishwanathan2024predicting, lin2025linking}. The accompanying studies of the MICrONS 
Consortium \cite{microns2025functional} extend this into mouse cortex, identifying a general 
wiring rule linking excitatory connection probability to functional similarity across layers 
and visual areas, and supplying a trained foundation model that predicts cortical responses 
to arbitrary visual stimuli \cite{wang2025foundation}. The thesis's connectome-as-reservoir 
framing belongs to this family. The specific commitment it adds, namely fixed recurrent 
weights with only a linear readout trained, evaluated against a graded null-model ladder, 
is set up in Section \ref{subsec: reservoir-computing} and applied to the 
connectome-reservoir literature in Section \ref{subsec: con-constrained-nets}.
 
The empirical data the thesis can draw on now spans a continuum. Cook et al. 
\cite{cook2019whole} provides the substrate of every experiment to date, with 
quantitative chemical and gap junction matrices for both sexes of C. elegans, edge weights 
obtained from counts of serial EM sections traversed by each synaptic structure, and a 
graph organised into three feedforward interneuron layers and a fourth cross-cutting layer; 
left-right variability of roughly 10 to 40 per cent in chemical edge weights, reported 
alongside an explicit framing of the matrices as a conceptualisation assembled across 
multiple animals, sets the empirical floor against which any null effect must be judged. 
The FlyWire reconstruction of Dorkenwald et al. \cite{dorkenwald2024neuronal} at 
approximately 140,000 neurons and the MICrONS mouse visual cortex at over 200,000 cells 
\cite{microns2025functional} extend this continuum at single-cell resolution into vertebrate 
cortex, with empirical synapse-resolved edge weights now a cross-scale standard and 
completeness now a continuum rather than a binary. Wilcox et al. \cite{wilcox2026network} 
test the same intuition at parcellated human macroscale, reporting that weak, long-range 
connections and small-world topology jointly predict a latent general-intelligence factor. 
The MICrONS volume in particular, as the only co-registered structural-functional cortical 
dataset, is the natural candidate substrate for the world-model stage at which Section 
\ref{subsec: world-models} closes the chapter.
 
Taken together, this literature establishes the connectome as a concept whose empirical 
realisation now spans the scale continuum the thesis works within, supplies multi-organism 
precedent for treating the connectome as a fixed weight matrix, and identifies which 
biological annotations carry computational consequence.
