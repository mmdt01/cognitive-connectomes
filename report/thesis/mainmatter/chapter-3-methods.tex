\section{The Connectivity Substrate}
\label{sec:methods-substrate}
\bigskip

The individual data are the 70 % METH A.1
diffusion-weighted datasets of a publicly released healthy-adult
cohort \cite{griffa2019structural}, in the form distributed with the
connectome-reservoir release of Suárez et al. \cite{suarez2021learning}. The
release provides the same cohort at several parcellation resolutions of the
Lausanne atlas. This thesis works at the two highest,
$N = 448$ and $N = 1000$. % METH A.4
Both of the measures Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-I} reports are percentiles taken
over a spectrum, and the reservoir measures in later chapters are ranks of an
$N$-dimensional state space, so each needs enough nodes to be informative; the
coarser resolutions do not supply them.

Each individual structural matrix is restricted to cortical parcels, and its
edge weights are streamline fibre densities. The group matrix is formed by a
distance-dependent consensus rule \cite{betzel2019distance}: candidate edges are
grouped into bins by the Euclidean distance between parcel centroids, with
inter-hemispheric and intra-hemispheric connections treated separately, and one
edge is retained per bin, the one present in the largest number of individuals.
A retained edge takes as its weight the mean of that connection across all
subjects. The result is symmetric, has a zero diagonal, and is non-negative
throughout, since tractography counts streamlines and cannot represent an
inhibitory connection. At $N = 448$ it holds
5{,}323 % METH A.6
undirected edges, a density of 5.32\%; % METH A.6
the same build at $N = 1000$ gives
10{,}784 % METH A.6
edges and 2.16\%. % METH A.6

\section{The null ladder}
\label{sec:methods-ladder}
\bigskip

Five null models and one control are used. In order of how much of the
connectome each keeps: 
a random gaussian matrix, which shares only the node count and the expected edge
density, its edges drawn independently at that density rather than fixed in
number; an Erdős–Rényi graph, matching the node
count, edge count and density exactly rather than in expectation; a
degree-preserving rewire, which adds the degree sequence and so carries the
connectome's heavy-tailed degree distribution intact; and
clustering-preserving and modularity-preserving rewires, each holding fixed
everything the degree rewire does and one further property of the topology
besides. Weights are painted onto every randomised graph by drawing, with
replacement, from the connectome's own multiset of edge
weights. % METH B.0
Each null is generated at ten seeds; % METH B.0
the connectome is one fixed graph and returns the same matrix every time.

The weight-permuted variant is a control rather than a null. It takes the
connectome's own graph and its own weights and permutes the weights across the
edges: same edge set, same degrees, and the same weight multiset exactly rather
than in distribution, since the values are reordered rather than
resampled. % METH B.1

Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-I} draws four of these seven: the connectome, the
weight-permuted control, Erdős–Rényi and the degree-preserving rewire, which is
forty cells at each parcellation. The clustering- and modularity-preserving
rewires appear there as numbers only, in Appendix~\ref{app:wider-ladder}. 
The random gaussian rung takes no part in that comparison either. It is not a
rung of the criticality-matched ladder, and its edge count is matched only in
expectation, so it is the one substrate here that does not hold the connectome's
edge count fixed.

\section{Validating the nulls}
\label{sec:methods-null-validation}
\bigskip

Each rung asserts its own defining property at construction, and those
assertions are weaker than they look. All of them test a property the generator
preserves by construction, % METH B.6
so they will catch a mis-wired pipeline, a transposed mask or a generator called
on the wrong graph, and none of them can report whether a randomisation has
actually explored anything.

The distinction matters for the degree-preserving rewire, the one rung whose
randomisation is iterative: its assertion holds after any number of swaps,
including none at all. That the chain has in fact mixed is a separate
measurement. At the production setting of ten accepted swaps per edge, the
rewired graph retains 3.6\% % A1 5.3
of the connectome's edges against a chance floor of roughly
2.2\% % A1 5.3
at this density, and retention is flat from five swaps per edge through to
forty, % A1 5.3
so the chain has settled well before the setting these results use.



\section{The bulk edge and the critical scale}
\label{sec:methods-bulk95}
\bigskip

The bulk edge used throughout is $\mathrm{bulk95}$: the 95th percentile of
$|\lambda|$ over the spectrum, divided by $|\lambda_1|$, so that it is expressed
as a fraction of the Perron root rather than in the units of the
weights. % A1 2.6 finding 1
The word outlier then invites one assumption that the definition does not
license. The Perron eigenvalue is inside the population the percentile is taken
over. % A1 2.6 finding 1
It is an outlier in where it sits, not one the statistic sets aside, and the
bulk edge is therefore a percentile of the whole spectrum rather than of the
spectrum with its largest element removed.

Every substrate-level $\mathrm{bulk95}$ reported in this thesis is the median
over seeds, and the critical scale derived from it is
$\mathrm{sr\_crit} = 1 / \mathrm{median}_{\text{seeds}}(\mathrm{bulk95})$. % TIER0 1.3
The median rather than the mean, because $1/x$ is convex, so the per-seed mean
of $1/\mathrm{bulk95}$ is biased upward against the inverse of the mean, by up
to 0.0431 % TIER0 1.3
within the four-substrate ladder. The median commutes with monotone transforms,
so the two computation orders agree and $\mathrm{sr\_crit}$ can be reproduced by
inverting the reported central $\mathrm{bulk95}$. % TIER0 1.3
The absolute bulk radius in raw units of $W$ is
$\mathrm{median}(\mathrm{bulk95}) \times \mathrm{median}(|\lambda_1|)$, the
product of the medians, which is the only aggregation under which the identity
$|\lambda_1| / \mathrm{abs\_bulk} = 1/\mathrm{bulk95} = \mathrm{sr\_crit}$
holds. % TIER0 3.1
