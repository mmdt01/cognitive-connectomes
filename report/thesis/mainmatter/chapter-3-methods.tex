\section{The Connectivity Substrate}
\label{sec:methods-substrate}
\bigskip

The individual data are the 70 % METH A.1
diffusion-weighted datasets of a publicly released healthy-adult
cohort \cite{griffa2019structural}, in the form distributed with the
connectome-reservoir release of Suárez et al. \cite{suarez2021learning}. The
release provides the same cohort at several parcellation resolutions of the
Lausanne atlas. This thesis works at the two highest,
$N = 448$ and $N = 1000$. % METH A.4
Both of the measures Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-I} reports are percentiles taken
over a spectrum, and the reservoir measures in later chapters are ranks of an
$N$-dimensional state space, so each needs enough nodes to be informative; the
coarser resolutions do not supply them.

Each individual structural matrix is restricted to cortical parcels, and its
edge weights are streamline fibre densities. The group matrix is formed by a
distance-dependent consensus rule \cite{betzel2019distance}: candidate edges are
grouped into bins by the Euclidean distance between parcel centroids, with
inter-hemispheric and intra-hemispheric connections treated separately, and one
edge is retained per bin, the one present in the largest number of individuals.
A retained edge takes as its weight the mean of that connection across all
subjects. The result is symmetric, has a zero diagonal, and is non-negative
throughout, since tractography counts streamlines and cannot represent an
inhibitory connection. At $N = 448$ it holds
5{,}323 % METH A.6
undirected edges, a density of 5.32\%; % METH A.6
the same build at $N = 1000$ gives
10{,}784 % METH A.6
edges and 2.16\%. % METH A.6

\section{The null ladder}
\label{sec:methods-ladder}
\bigskip

Five null models and one control are used. In order of how much of the
connectome each keeps: 
a random gaussian matrix, which shares only the node count and the expected edge
density, its edges drawn independently at that density rather than fixed in
number; an Erdős–Rényi graph, matching the node
count, edge count and density exactly rather than in expectation; a
degree-preserving rewire, which adds the degree sequence and so carries the
connectome's heavy-tailed degree distribution intact; and
clustering-preserving and modularity-preserving rewires, each holding fixed
everything the degree rewire does and one further property of the topology
besides. Weights are painted onto every randomised graph by drawing, with
replacement, from the connectome's own multiset of edge
weights. % METH B.0
Each null is generated at ten seeds; % METH B.0
the connectome is one fixed graph and returns the same matrix every time.

The weight-permuted variant is a control rather than a null. It takes the
connectome's own graph and its own weights and permutes the weights across the
edges: same edge set, same degrees, and the same weight multiset exactly rather
than in distribution, since the values are reordered rather than
resampled. % METH B.1

Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-I} draws four of these seven: the connectome, the
weight-permuted control, Erdős–Rényi and the degree-preserving rewire, which is
forty cells at each parcellation. The clustering- and modularity-preserving
rewires appear there as numbers only, in Appendix~\ref{app:wider-ladder}. 
The random gaussian rung takes no part in that comparison either. It is not a
rung of the criticality-matched ladder, and its edge count is matched only in
expectation, so it is the one substrate here that does not hold the connectome's
edge count fixed.

\section{Validating the nulls}
\label{sec:methods-null-validation}
\bigskip

Each rung asserts its own defining property at construction, and those
assertions are weaker than they look. All of them test a property the generator
preserves by construction, % METH B.6
so they will catch a mis-wired pipeline, a transposed mask or a generator called
on the wrong graph, and none of them can report whether a randomisation has
actually explored anything.

The distinction matters for the degree-preserving rewire, the one rung whose
randomisation is iterative: its assertion holds after any number of swaps,
including none at all. That the chain has in fact mixed is a separate
measurement. At the production setting of ten accepted swaps per edge, the
rewired graph retains 3.8\% % A1 5.3
of the connectome's edges against a chance floor of roughly
2.2\% % A1 5.3
at this density, and retention is flat from five swaps per edge through to
forty, % A1 5.3
so the chain has settled well before the setting these results use.



\section{The bulk edge and the critical scale}
\label{sec:methods-bulk95}
\bigskip

The bulk edge used throughout is $\mathrm{bulk95}$: the 95th percentile of
$|\lambda|$ over the spectrum, divided by $|\lambda_1|$, so that it is expressed
as a fraction of the Perron root rather than in the units of the
weights. % A1 2.6 finding 1
The word outlier then invites one assumption that the definition does not
license. The Perron eigenvalue is inside the population the percentile is taken
over. % A1 2.6 finding 1
It is an outlier in where it sits, not one the statistic sets aside, and the
bulk edge is therefore a percentile of the whole spectrum rather than of the
spectrum with its largest element removed.

Every substrate-level $\mathrm{bulk95}$ reported in this thesis is the median
over seeds, and the critical scale derived from it is
$\mathrm{sr\_crit} = 1 / \mathrm{median}_{\text{seeds}}(\mathrm{bulk95})$. % TIER0 1.3
The median rather than the mean, because $1/x$ is convex, so the per-seed mean
of $1/\mathrm{bulk95}$ is biased upward against the inverse of the mean, by up
to 0.0431 % TIER0 1.3
within the four-substrate ladder. The median commutes with monotone transforms,
so the two computation orders agree and $\mathrm{sr\_crit}$ can be reproduced by
inverting the reported central $\mathrm{bulk95}$. % TIER0 1.3
The absolute bulk radius in raw units of $W$ is
$\mathrm{median}(\mathrm{bulk95}) \times \mathrm{median}(|\lambda_1|)$, the
product of the medians, which is the only aggregation under which the identity
$|\lambda_1| / \mathrm{abs\_bulk} = 1/\mathrm{bulk95} = \mathrm{sr\_crit}$
holds. % TIER0 3.1




\section{The comparison problem: two matching axes}
\label{sec:methods-matching}
\bigskip
% Carries A1.6. Figure F3. Named in CROSS_ACT_SPINE.md under Act I as the
% section Act I owns in chapter 3; Chapters 5 and 6 carry it forward rather
% than re-deriving it. A1.7 is NOT claimed here: see the audit note.
% FORBIDDEN: "compact bulk"; the crossing quoted without its axis; any claim
% that one axis is the correct one; "the connectome is a better reservoir".

Comparing substrates requires putting them at the same operating point, and on
these substrates there is no single way to do it. The reason is
Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-I}'s result. The four absolute bulk radii agree to
4.4\% % TIER0 3.1
of their mean while their Perron roots run from
0.1061 to 0.1889, % TIER0 3.1
so the substrates differ in essentially one quantity, and a matching rule must
choose which end of the spectrum it holds fixed.

Scaling each substrate to a nominal spectral radius $\sigma$ divides it by its
own $|\lambda_1|$, which sets the leading eigenvalue to $\sigma$ exactly and
leaves the bulk where it falls. It is the normalisation the connectome-reservoir
literature uses \cite{suarez2021learning, suarez2024connectome,
milisav2026neuromorphic}. Under it the four bulks, expressed as fractions of
their own Perron roots, spread
47.3\%: % TIER0 3.1
the same bulks that agreed to 4.4\% in raw units. Nothing about them has moved
between those two numbers, only the divisor, and the divisor is the one quantity
the comparison is about. A nominal-matched comparison therefore holds the Perron
root fixed and lets each substrate sit at a different distance from its own
$\mathrm{sr\_crit}$.

The alternative is the product $\sigma \cdot \mathrm{bulk95}$, which holds the
bulk edge fixed instead: at $\sigma \cdot \mathrm{bulk95} = 1$ every substrate's
bulk edge reaches one, whatever its Perron root. That axis exists because each
substrate arrives carrying its own $\mathrm{bulk95}$, and it is available only
for that reason.

Neither axis is neutral, and it is worth being explicit about why not, because
the temptation is to treat the second as a correction to the first. Each holds
fixed exactly what the other lets vary. Since the substrates differ in one
quantity, no third axis holds both fixed at once, and the choice is therefore a
choice about which hypothesis is being given a hearing rather than a choice
between a naive convention and its repair. A comparison made on the nominal axis
is a comparison at matched Perron roots and unmatched bulks; one made on the
matched-bulk axis is the reverse. Neither is the neutral view, and there is no
neutral view to be had.

Two consequences follow, and both are enforced throughout this thesis.
\textbf{Every result is quoted with the axis it was measured on}, and results on
the two axes are never set against each other as though they disagreed: they are
two measurements of different things. And \textbf{the two axes have different
coverage}, because holding the bulk fixed compresses the reachable range of
operating points by each substrate's own $\mathrm{bulk95}$. A sweep that runs to
$\sigma = 8$ on the nominal axis reaches much less far in the matched
coordinate, so a comparison on that axis is bounded by where the substrates'
ranges still overlap, and a feature at the edge of that overlap must not be read
as though the sweep continued past it.

Figure~\ref{fig:F3} shows what this costs on a real result, using the memory
measure Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-II} develops. The same two substrates, the same
recorded states, read on the two axes: a large advantage on one, a smaller one on
the other, and a subcritical deficit that is almost entirely an artefact of the
normalisation. It is placed here rather than with that result because it is not
a finding about the connectome. It is what the comparison itself does, and
Chapters~\ref{chapter:act-II} and~\ref{chapter:act-III} carry it forward rather
than re-deriving it.

\section{The state matrix and its Gram spectrum}
\label{sec:methods-states}
\bigskip
% Landing for chapter 5 cut items 2, 5, 6 and 14: the Gram matrix as an object,
% the centring identity's derivation and implementation, and the reasons no
% link in Chapter 5 is derived from W's spectrum.

Every measure of activity in this thesis is a function of one object. Driving a
reservoir with an input signal and recording every unit at every timestep gives
the state matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\mathrm{eff}} \times N}$, a trajectory
through an $N$-dimensional space with one point per timestep, whose shape is what
the phrase \emph{activity manifold} refers to. Runs are
3000 timesteps with the first 500 discarded as warmup, % A2 2.5
so $T_{\mathrm{eff}} = 2500$. % A2 2.5
The memory-capacity design carries no bias column, so its Gram spectrum has
448 entries; % A2 2.5
the two prediction tasks carry one and have 449. % A2 2.5

A linear readout does not see the trajectory. It sees the matrix of pairwise
inner products between the units' time series, the Gram matrix $G = A^{\top}A$,
whose entry $(i,j)$ is unit $i$'s time series dotted with unit $j$'s. That matrix
records how the units co-vary and nothing else, fixing the shape of the
trajectory up to a rotation: two networks whose states have the same Gram matrix
have the same activity manifold in every respect a readout could detect, however
differently the two are wired. It is also, literally, the matrix a ridge readout
inverts, since the fit is
$\hat{w} = (G + \alpha I)^{-1}A^{\top}y$. The companion object $AA^{\top}$
describes the geometry over time rather than over units and is where the shape of
the trajectory as a path lives; the two share their non-zero eigenvalues, so the
phrase \emph{the Gram spectrum} is unambiguous, and it is the first that is
measured here.

Writing $m = T_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1}A^{\top}\mathbf{1}$ for each unit's mean over
time and $\tilde{A} = A - \mathbf{1}m^{\top}$ for the time-centred states,
expanding $A^{\top}A$ gives Equation~\ref{eq:gram-split} exactly: the
fluctuations about the mean plus a rank-one term holding the time-average.
Centring subtracts each unit's mean \emph{over time}, not the mean over units,
and the implementation agrees with a direct covariance computation to
$3.3 \times 10^{-16}$. % A2 2.6 finding 2
Which of the two objects is in use is not a preprocessing detail, because it
changes what the leading eigenvector means, and every measure below is labelled
with the one it is computed on. The participation ratio is computed on the
time-centred covariance. The ridge effective rank is computed on the un-centred
Gram matrix, deliberately, because the un-centred matrix is the one the solver
inverts.

One thing is not attempted anywhere in this thesis. The position of a Gram
spectrum is never obtained analytically from the eigenvalues of $W$. The
nonlinearity and the statistics of the input both intervene, and the same
recurrent matrix driven at different operating points puts its Gram spectrum in
materially different places, so the link Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-II} draws
between the spectral gap and the number of usable directions is measured one step
at a time rather than derived.

A last approximation is worth bounding, since the memory-capacity design is
nested across lags while the Gram spectrum is recorded once. Weyl's inequality
bounds the drop in $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ from lag zero to lag $k$ by $k$ itself,
which over the fifty lags used here permits a shift of up to
50 units; % A2 2.5
measured on real cells the worst shift is
0.31 to 0.82 units, % A2 2.5
which is 1.6\% % A2 2.5
of that bound. The recorded value is therefore a valid stand-in at every lag, and
the approximation cannot move an ordering.


\section{Two counts of dimensionality}
\label{sec:methods-counts}
\bigskip
% Landing for chapter 5 cut items 1 and 8: the justification of d_eff, the
% terminology collision, the participation ratio's definition, and the ridge
% regularisation strengths. Chapter 5 defines both measures and argues between
% them; what is here is why each is the quantity it is.

The ridge effective rank of Equation~\ref{eq:deff} is not a descriptor applied to
a fit from outside. Written in trace form,
$d_{\mathrm{eff}}(\alpha) = \operatorname{tr}\bigl[G(G + \alpha I)^{-1}\bigr]$,
it is the effective degrees of freedom of the fitted readout in the standard
sense~\cite{hastie2009elements}: a property of the fit itself. That is what makes
it the natural count here rather than one choice among many. The alternatives
available by inspection are all worse for this substrate family. These Gram
spectra decay smoothly across ten orders of magnitude without reaching zero, so a
numerical-rank criterion returns $N$ for every substrate, and a hand-placed
threshold would count two otherwise indistinguishable directions as one and as
none. Both treat availability as a property of the states alone, when whether a
direction is available depends on what is reading them.

The name collides with an established one. \emph{Effective rank} is also used for
a quantity computed on the recurrent matrix
itself~\cite{clark2025connectivity}. That is a property of $W$; the measure used
here is computed on the states the network produces when driven, at a stated
operating point, by a stated input. The two are related, since the states are the
network's, but they are not the same measurement and no result of this thesis
transfers between them.

The regularisation strength is the readout's own. For the memory-capacity task it
is $\alpha = 10^{-6}$, % TIER0 3.3
taken from the same parameter dictionary that configures the evaluator rather
than set separately for the measure, so the two are the same float and not two
copies of one number. The prediction tasks carry
$10^{-8}$ and $10^{-7}$. % CHK Task 2
This identity is load-bearing: the correspondence between $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and
measured memory capacity holds at
+0.999 % TIER0 3.3
at every regularisation strength from $10^{-8}$ to $10^{-3}$, and reading one
through the other is licensed only while the two values agree. Raising $\alpha$
does not break the link provided it is raised in both places. One implementation
detail follows from the bias column: the routine regularises the unregularised
bias direction, which is bounded by
one $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ unit % A2 2.6 finding 5
and is in practice around $10^{-12}$. The memory-capacity design has no bias
column, so nothing in Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-II} is affected.

The participation ratio is the standard instrument for the same question in
systems neuroscience. Given the eigenvalues $\mu_1 \ge \dots \ge \mu_N$ of the
time-centred state covariance, the variance the population puts along each
direction,
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{PR}
    \;=\; \frac{\bigl(\sum_i \mu_i\bigr)^{2}}{\sum_i \mu_i^{2}} ,
    \label{eq:pr}
\end{equation}
which equals the number of directions when variance is spread evenly across them
and falls toward one as a few directions come to dominate. It has reasons behind
it beyond convenience, being closely related to the quantities that govern how
population dimensionality is recovered from finite
recordings~\cite{gao2017theory}. Section~\ref{sec:act2-counting} compares the two
measures on the same states; what is recorded here is only that they are computed
on the same recorded run, one before centring and one after.


\section{Aggregation conventions for the manifold measures}
\label{sec:methods-aggregation}
\bigskip
% Landing for chapter 5 cut items 4 and 14: the sign convention on the
% common-mode amplitude, the supercritical filter as applied to artifacts, the
% zero-strip, and the inventory of captures.

The common-mode amplitude is signed, and its sign is arbitrary: which way the
pattern points is settled by the input realisation a given run happens to draw,
so runs of the same substrate straddle zero. The absolute value is therefore
taken per run, \textbf{before} any aggregation, never after. The order is not a
matter of taste. Combining first can only understate the amplitude, by an amount
that depends on how many of a substrate's runs straddle zero, which differs
between substrates: in the wrong order the connectome's value at $\sigma = 6$
falls from 0.759 % TIER0 3.7
to 0.638, % TIER0 3.12
while the weight-permuted control falls to
0.575, % A2 5.1
below the connectome, reversing the comparison
Section~\ref{sec:act2-perron} draws. The largest discrepancy between the two
orders anywhere in the sweep is
0.676. % A2 5.1
The figure builders assert the correct order rather than relying on it.

Substrate-level values of every manifold measure are medians over seeds.
\emph{Supercritical} statistics are medians over the fifty cells per substrate at
an operating point of at least
3.05, % TIER0 3.2
which is five radii of the measurement grid at ten seeds each. Where a statistic
is a count of Gram eigenvalues, exact zeros are dropped before the statistic is
formed; % TIER0 3.6(a)
the count of exact zeros grows down the null ladder, from a median of
1 for the connectome to 91.5 for Erdős–Rényi, % TIER0 3.6(a)
so the convention is load-bearing rather than cosmetic, and
Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-II} reports exact zeros as their own category rather
than folding them in.
