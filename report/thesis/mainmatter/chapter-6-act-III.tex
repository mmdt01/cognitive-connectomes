% P1: what arrives, and the boundary that arrives with it.
Three things arrive from Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-II}, and none of them is a
statement about what a network can compute. The first is the \textbf{common
mode}, the pattern in which every unit moves together: the previous chapter
established that a non-negative substrate cannot avoid carrying one, and
measured how far each substrate is dominated by its own
(Section~\ref{sec:act2-perron}). It arrives as a description of what the states
look like, not as an explanation of anything they do. The second is the \textbf{ridge
effective rank} $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (Equation~\ref{eq:deff}), which counts the
directions of the state matrix a linear readout can act on. It arrives earned
rather than assumed, and with its scope attached: it is the right quantity for
asking how much of a manifold a readout can use and the wrong one for asking
what shape that manifold has (Section~\ref{sec:act2-counting}). The third is the
reason the second must be read more than once. Where a substrate's Gram spectrum
sits relative to the readout's regularisation floor is not fixed by the
substrate but moves with how hard the network is driven
(Section~\ref{sec:act2-floor}), so \textbf{usable dimensionality is a property of a
substrate at an operating point}. A fourth thing arrives and it is a
boundary rather than a result. On non-negative weights the shape of the
trajectory does not move across the operating-point sweep while predictive
performance falls sharply, so capacity is lost there with the geometry intact,
and what governs that loss was left open and named as open
(Section~\ref{sec:act2-temporal}). This chapter does not narrow itself around
that boundary; Section~\ref{sec:act3-prediction-sigma} is where it is measured.

% P2: the two demands, and the third that joins them.
What a substrate is asked for is not one quantity. A readout can be asked to
reconstruct an input the network has already seen, which requires that traces of
the past still be separable in the present state; or it can be asked to
sustain a signal on its own, driving itself from its own output with no further
input to correct it. The first is a demand on how many independent directions
survive; the second is a demand on whether the dynamics stay in a regime where a
trajectory can be continued at all. This chapter puts both to the same
substrates, along the same axis, and its unifying question is whether the two
answers are two facts or one. Which task carries which demand was fixed in
advance, before any of the data below was inspected, so that no claim can be
rescued by the observation that there is always some task on which it holds.
Retention is carried by the \textbf{memory-capacity} task, with NARMA-10 and
Mackey-Glass as corroboration. Generation is carried by the \textbf{Lorenz prediction}
task, and it has \emph{no} corroborating task: the regime switch and the
resistance margin reported below rest on Lorenz alone. The joint question is
carried by the two together, and what that costs is returned to in
Section~\ref{sec:act3-scope}.

% P3: the primary variable, stated concretely.
Both demands are made along one variable. Every substrate in this thesis is run
as the operator $\sigma W/|\lambda_1|$, whose spectral radius is exactly $\sigma$
for every variant by construction and whose bulk radius is
$\sigma \cdot \mathrm{bulk95}$. That $\sigma$ is the \textbf{operating point}:
how hard the recurrent dynamics are driven, and the one quantity in this
comparison that has a biological counterpart a brain actually moves, through
neuromodulation, arousal and plasticity. It is the primary variable of this
thesis and every headline claim in this chapter is made along it. The regimes
the comparison is run across, subcritical, near-critical and supercritical, are
positions on that variable, and the supercritical window used throughout is the
shared one defined by the connectome's own critical scale, which means it is not
symmetric between substrates: at the top of it each null is running far past its
own critical scale while the connectome has only just reached its.

Two matching axes follow from that normalisation, and
Chapter~\ref{chapter:methods} establishes both
(Figure~\ref{fig:f3-two-axes}). Matching on nominal $\sigma$ holds the
spectral radius fixed and lets the bulk radius vary; matching on
$\sigma\!\cdot\!\mathrm{bulk95}$ holds the bulk radius fixed and leaves the
Perron root deliberately unmatched. Neither choice is neutral, and the second
is the less neutral of the two in this chapter: the mechanism under test is
the Perron mode, and at the matched point the connectome is run at
$\sigma = 6.0$ against Erdős--Rényi's % TIER0 1.1
$3.54$, % TIER0 1.1
a Perron root $1.7$ times larger, % TIER0 1.1
so that axis hands the connectome more of the very quantity proposed as the
cause. The subcritical deficit those two axes disagree about is present at
matched spectral radius and absent at matched bulk radius, which is why every
result below is reported on both and rests on surviving both.

Two scope statements close this section. The first is that
Sections~\ref{sec:act3-memory-sigma} and~\ref{sec:act3-prediction-sigma} are
measured entirely on non-negative weights, and that a manipulation is introduced
in Section~\ref{sec:act3-lesion-memory}: the sign fraction $f$, the proportion of
weights made negative. It enters as an intervention and not as a second axis of
the chapter. Macroscale diffusion tractography cannot represent the sign of a
connection, so $f = 0$ is what the instrument produces rather than a property of
cortex, and grading $f$ upward asks what the missing half of the biology would
do. The second concerns how the results below are to be read against one
another. They are measured at operating points that differ by more than a
factor of three, and the operating point is not a detail of the setup but the
variable the chapter is about: the connectome's resistance to collapse appears
far above its own critical scale, at $\sigma \approx 7.6$ to $8.0$, % TIER0 2.3
while the generative advantage over the nulls is measured near criticality, at
$\sigma = 2$. % TIER0 2.6
Quoted without its operating point, either statement makes the other look false.
Every claim below therefore names the operating point it is measured at and the
axis it is matched on, and none should be read without them.

A reservoir's memory is usually reported as a single number, the best it
achieves anywhere along the operating-point axis, and that number cannot answer
the question this section asks. Measured as the ridge effective rank
(Equation~\ref{eq:deff}), every substrate on the ladder peaks within a few
percent of the hard $d_{\mathrm{eff}} = N$ ceiling, % TIER0 1.2
because at its own optimum each is using very nearly every direction available
to it; the larger network reported below does not escape that ceiling either.
Peak capacity is therefore not resolvable at this size, and no claim is made
about it in either direction. What is resolvable is what happens on either side
of the peak. A ceiling can clip curves but it cannot manufacture a crossing
between them, so a difference in how fast substrates fall away from their optima
is robust to finite size in a way a difference between the optima is not. This
section reads the decay region, and the result it finds there is a crossing.

% P2: the crossing. Claim A3M.1. Figure F7(a, c).
% Numerals: 7. The three null peak values are F7's caption's, not the body's:
% the ordering within the nulls is not a claim at any scale (sheet 14).
On the axis that holds the bulk radius fixed, all four substrates rise together,
all four reach the ceiling, and they separate only on the way down
(Figure~\ref{fig:F7}). The connectome's peak is the lowest of the four,
at $432.4$ of a possible $448$; % TIER0 1.2
its three nulls peak above it. But at the top of the four-variant overlap, at
$\sigma \cdot \mathrm{bulk95} = 2.599$, % TIER0 1.2
which is the largest matched radius at which every substrate and every seed
still has coverage, the connectome retains
$47\%$ % TIER0 1.2
of its own peak against the nulls'
$28$, $22$ and $11\%$: % TIER0 1.2
a four-fold spread in what survives, in the reverse of the order in which the
substrates peaked. The ordering the peak gives and the ordering the decay gives
are opposite, and that crossing, rather than either ordering alone, is the
finding. It is not a property of the axis that produces it: the same retention
ordering holds on the nominal axis, where the Perron root rather than the bulk
is what is held fixed (Figure~\ref{fig:F3}), % TIER0 1.2
so it survives the matching choice that hands the connectome the larger gap.
What it is not is a global advantage. The ladder ordering is regional: it runs
the other way below criticality, where the nominal axis leaves the bulk
unmatched, it is absent near the peak, and above criticality it is strong and
consistent at $+0.93$. % TIER0 3.2
Stated on the axis that holds the bulk fixed, where the subcritical deficit is
not present at all, the defensible summary is parity below criticality and
advantage above it.

statement about their similarity, and that comparison is worth making precisely
because it runs against the chapter's direction. Paired within seed, each
substrate at its own optimum and each pair sharing input weights and input
series, the connectome's peak memory capacity sits
$2$ to $6\%$ % TIER0 3.4
below the nulls' (Figure~\ref{fig:F10}). The intervals are what make that
readable. Against Erdős--Rényi and against the weight-permuted placement control
the difference excludes zero at every ridge strength tested; against
degree-matching it does so at
one of five, % TIER0 3.4
and the point estimate changes sign at one of them. The defensible statement is
therefore \emph{parity} at the peak, not a deficit, and certainly not that this
substrate is the worst of the four. That the five ridge strengths span five
orders of magnitude makes the panel a robustness check rather than a search: the
correspondence between usable dimensionality and measured memory holds at every
one of them, provided the regularisation is raised in both places at once, so
the readout's regularisation is not what produces the result in either
direction. The same panel is where an apparent disagreement with the literature
resolves. At the smallest ridge, nearest the pseudoinverse limit that work
optimises, the reported spread-beats-compact ordering reproduces exactly on
these substrates, with Erdős--Rényi highest and the connectome
lowest~\cite{aceituno2020tailoring}. It is reproduced rather than contradicted, and the
two results are about different places on the axis: spread wins at the peak, and
a large spectral gap wins across the range.

What the gap buys is visible in the quantity the previous chapter measured. A
non-negative matrix has an all-positive leading eigenvector by
Perron--Frobenius, so every substrate here carries a common mode, and driving
the network harder drives that mode harder. The nulls give way to it. At the
supercritical operating point $\sigma = 6$ their common-mode amplitude stands at
$0.949$ to $0.989$, % TIER0 3.7
which is very nearly every unit pinned at the
same saturated value, doing the same thing at the same time. The connectome
reaches $0.759$, % TIER0 3.7
the least dominated of the four, and it does so while carrying by far the
largest Perron root of the four: the substrate with the most to be dominated by
is the one least dominated. The amplitude climbs with the operating point for
every substrate, and the connectome's climb starts lower and ends lower
(Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-II}, Figure~\ref{fig:F4}). That is what a
large spectral gap does here. It lets the leading mode be driven hard without
the bulk of the spectrum following it, so the activity that is not the common
mode survives, and Section~\ref{sec:act2-floor} is what connects that to the
measure this section reads: what stays clear of the readout's regularisation
floor is what the readout can use, and supercritically the connectome holds
$89.0\%$ of its directions more than a decade clear of that floor against
Erdős--Rényi's $11.4\%$. % TIER0 3.6
The decay difference in Figure~\ref{fig:F7} is that gap, read out.

% Numerals: 0. The last clause hands 6.4 its motivation without naming f as a

Put in one line, the finding is that the connectome does not make memory better.
Non-negativity makes memory worse for every substrate, and the connectome's
spectral gap makes it least worse. Both halves of that sentence carry a cost and
neither is left for a reader to find. The first is that at its own optimum this
substrate is not ahead, and the interval says so. The second is sharper: on the
quantity the reservoir literature has largely optimised, peak capacity at
minimal regularisation, this substrate loses, and the ordering that says so is
reproduced above rather than explained away. The reply is not that the
measurement is wrong but that the operating point is not a free parameter. A
tuned reservoir can be placed at its optimum and held there; a brain is driven
across a range by neuromodulation, arousal and plasticity, and what matters over
a range is how much is retained away from the optimum rather than how much is
available at it. That is the sense in which the advantage here is robustness and
not capacity. It also points at what would test the account rather than
illustrate it. Every substrate compared so far has a common mode, because every
one has non-negative weights, so comparison alone cannot show that the common
mode is what the difference is made of. The next section removes it.

The two demands of this chapter are not scored the same way. A driven task is
corrected at every step, because the input the network receives is the true one,
so an error made at one step does not propagate into the state scored at the
next. A free-running network has no such correction: it is initialised on a true
trajectory, released, and thereafter driven by its own output, so errors compound
and what is scored is how long the generated trajectory stays with the true one
before leaving it. That duration, expressed in Lyapunov times so that it is a
property of the dynamics rather than of the integration step, is the valid
prediction time. One consequence of the arrangement must be stated plainly or
everything below is misread: trajectory curvature is measured under teacher
forcing, on the driven trajectory, while valid prediction time is measured on the
free run. This is not a defect, since the loss of generative capacity is a
property of the operator and is visible under drive, but the two are not

$7.2\%$ % A3P 2.6
of evaluations at $f = 0$ score exactly zero, meaning the generated trajectory
left the true one at the first predicted step rather than drifting away from it.

$4.43$ to $0.44$ % TIER0 3.11

$\sigma = 8$ % TIER0 3.11

$p = 0.033$). % TIER0 2.3

statement holds only where it is made. Near criticality, at $\sigma = 2$, where
every substrate is close to its own best prediction, the same comparison at the

$+0.28$, $-0.01$ and $+0.44$ % TIER0 2.6

which there are ten per substrate, and repeated draws at $f = 0$ are duplicates
rather than additional evidence. With ten units the resolution of any test is
bounded from below regardless of the effect, so the claim rests on the size and
direction of the asymmetry, five against zero, and its family of comparisons is
declared narrowly rather than expanded afterwards.

cruder measure the ordering reverses. Counting substrates by how many of their
seeds stop predicting altogether at some point in the upper sweep, the connectome
is the worst of the four and the degree-matched null is the best, with no failing

$\sigma = 3.6$, % AUDIT A2, A5
against two of Erdős--Rényi's. % AUDIT A3

confined to the upper sweep and none occurs at or below the operating point where
the near-critical comparison is made. They are never a whole condition failing:
at worst half the seeds of a given condition score zero and the rest predict at
between one and three Lyapunov times, in line with their neighbours. And they are
not the same event as the period-two collapse, which is recorded in a separate
artifact that carries no per-run identifier, so the two cannot be matched seed by
seed and no relation between them is asserted here. What can be said is narrower
than it first appears: the connectome resists one specific mode of generative
failure, and it is not the most reliable substrate on this task.

% P4: three candidates, each ruled out on the data. Numerals: 6.

What sets generation at $f = 0$ is then a well-posed question with three obvious
answers, and the data rules out all three. The first is geometry, and
Figure~\ref{fig:decay-dissociation} has already disposed of it: the quantity that
would have to register a graded loss does not move for the substrate whose
capacity falls ten-fold, and where it moves for others it moves as a jump between
two branches, which is not what a graded account predicts. The second is an
effective radius. A driven network's operator can be linearised about its own
state and assigned an effective spectral radius, $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}$, which
Section~\ref{sec:act3-lesion-generation} develops as the best empirical locator
of where generation breaks. At $f = 0$ it does not apply. Erdős--Rényi's
transition occurs at
$\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.014$, % TIER0 3.10
two orders of magnitude below the largest value it reaches anywhere on the sweep
and on the descending branch of its own curve, so nothing is being crossed; and
the connectome does not break at $f = 0$ at all within
$\sigma \leq 11.2$, % TIER0 3.11
so for that substrate there is no transition to locate. The third is that memory
is doing the work, generation being retention run forward. The ordering is
inverted. At $\sigma = 6$ the connectome holds a memory capacity of
$11.43$ against Erdős--Rényi's $2.42$, % TIER0 3.11
and a slightly \emph{lower} valid prediction time,
$0.81$ against $1.18$. % TIER0 3.11
The substrate with far more of the past available to it predicts the future
slightly less well.

% P5: the question left open and named as open. Claim A3P.9. Numerals: 1.
% Must not resolve by implication, and must not let 6.5's mechanism read as
% general. F17 deliberately not named.
No fourth candidate is offered here. Two further facts say why this is a boundary
of the present work rather than an omission from it. The first is that the map
governing the closed loop is not the recurrent matrix but that matrix plus a
rank-three term formed from the input and output weights, and the evaluator used
throughout this thesis does not expose the fitted readout, so the operator that
actually generates the trajectory was never available to be measured. The second
is that the failure is not a local linear bifurcation: the smallest eigenvalue of
the exact Jacobian at the fixed point never approaches
$-1$ % TIER0 3.11
anywhere in the sweep, so generation breaks while the fixed point it breaks away
from is still linearly stable. A mean-field correction was the obvious next
refinement and it is not the missing piece either. What remains is a capacity
lost with the geometry intact, on the only sign configuration the instrument can
produce, and no account of what governs it. The sections that follow introduce an
intervention and find a mechanism that operates under it; that mechanism is not an
answer to this question, and Section~\ref{sec:act3-scope} states the limit
explicitly rather than leaving it to be inferred.

% P1: why an intervention is needed, and what this one is. Numerals: 0.
The comparison of Section~\ref{sec:act3-memory-sigma} cannot settle its own
mechanism. Every substrate on the ladder is non-negative, so by
Perron--Frobenius every one of them has an all-positive leading eigenvector and
therefore a common mode, and a comparison between four substrates that all carry
the same feature cannot establish that the feature is what separates them. The
account offered there was that a large spectral gap lets the leading mode be
driven hard without the bulk of the spectrum following it. If that is right, then
taking the common mode away should take the difference with it. This section does
exactly that. A fraction $f$ of the weights is made negative, leaving the
magnitudes untouched and the topology intact, so that what changes between one
network and the next is the sign pattern and nothing else. As $f$ approaches one
half the excitatory and inhibitory contributions to each unit's input come into
balance and the all-positive leading direction has nothing left to be built from.
The manipulation is not a perturbation away from biology. Macroscale diffusion
tractography cannot represent the sign of a connection, so a non-negative matrix
is what the instrument produces rather than what cortex is, and increasing $f$
restores the half of the biology the measurement cannot see. It enters here as an
intervention on a substrate, not as a second axis of the chapter: every operating
point in this section is held fixed. 
\footnote{Which edges are inverted is controlled rather than left to chance.
Edges are ranked by how central their two endpoints are in the network and
divided into ten equal groups, and the same proportion is inverted at random
within each, so that the inverted set has the same hub-to-periphery composition
as the graph at every $f$. The alternative, drawing uniformly from all edges,
would let the amount of sign and its placement vary together: inverting an edge
between two hubs disturbs the leading eigenvector far more than inverting a
peripheral one, so at a fixed $f$ the spectral effect would depend on how many
hub edges the draw happened to catch. Chapter~\ref{chapter:methods} gives the
procedure in full.}

% P2: the common mode goes, and it goes for everyone. Claim A3M.5, F11(a).
% Numerals: 5.
The intervention does what it was designed to do, and does it to every substrate
alike. Held at the supercritical operating point
$\sigma = 6$, % TIER0 3.7
where Section~\ref{sec:act3-memory-sigma} found the substrates furthest apart,
the common-mode amplitude with non-negative weights stands at
$0.949$ to $0.989$ % TIER0 3.7
for the three nulls and at
$0.759$ % TIER0 3.7
for the connectome (Figure~\ref{fig:F11}a). As $f$ rises it falls for all four,
and by the time the signs are balanced at
$f = 0.5$ % TIER0 3.7
it has fallen by two orders of magnitude on every substrate. The four curves
converge as they fall, because the connectome starts nearest the floor and has
least distance to travel: the nulls lose a feature that dominated their activity,
and the connectome loses one it was already largely free of. What the sign
balance removes is therefore not something the connectome had and the nulls
lacked. It is the handicap the nulls carried and the connectome did not.

% P3: what goes with it, and the restatement that matters. Numerals: 6.
% "Nobody degrades" is the load-bearing correction here: memory RISES with f for

If the common mode is what the ladder was made of, then removing it should remove
the ladder, and it does. The wedge between the connectome and the nulls narrows
as $f$ rises and has closed by
$f \approx 0.15$ to $0.20$. % TIER0 2.6
The way it closes is the finding, and it is not the way the word "removal"
suggests. No substrate is harmed by the intervention. Supercritical memory
capacity rises with $f$ for all four, the connectome's from
$11.43$ to $14.18$ % TIER0 2.6
and Erdős--Rényi's from
$2.42$ to $13.11$ % TIER0 2.6

because the nulls catch up, and they catch up from far below because they had
much further to come. That is the prediction the common-mode account makes and
the alternative accounts do not: on a reading in which the connectome's placement
confers some intrinsic computational property, balancing the signs should have
degraded the substrate that had the property, and instead it lifted the ones that
lacked it. Read together with the peak comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:act3-memory-sigma}, the supercritical advantage is a rescue from
a handicap that non-negativity imposes on everyone, and not a capacity the
connectome supplies.

Section~\ref{sec:act3-lesion-memory} removed the common mode and the memory
ladder dissolved with it. The same intervention applied to generation does
something else entirely, and seeing why requires one fact about what a negative
weight does dynamically. Each unit in the network is a saturating map of its own
recurrent input: it takes the weighted sum reaching it, adds the drive, and
passes the result through a $\tanh$. Considered on its own, such a map has a
stable fixed point when its effective gain is sufficiently positive and a stable
period-two orbit when the gain is sufficiently negative, and between those two
regimes it has no stable behaviour at all. With non-negative weights only the
first branch is reachable. Making a fraction of the weights negative opens the
second. The consequence for this section is that the intervention does not turn a
dial: it makes a second dynamical regime available, and a network either enters it
or does not. Everything below follows from that, and the first thing to check is
whether the geometry behaves as the map says it should.

mean gain the units are running at, reaches unity. It does not. Only
$1$ of $38$ % TIER0 3.10
brackets around observed transitions contains unity, and the transitions
concentrate between
$0.77$ and $0.90$. % TIER0 3.10
Independently of that, the effective radius folds as signs are balanced, so across
a whole range of sign fractions its largest attainable value lies below unity
while transitions keep happening, and a threshold that cannot be reached in a
regime where the event still occurs is not a stability law. What survives is
weaker and still useful. Of the three candidate rulers available it is the most
invariant across substrates and sign fractions, its interquartile range being
$0.209$ % TIER0 3.10
times its median against the nominal operating point's
$0.667$, % TIER0 3.10
so it locates the transition well without defining it. One thing was predicted
before it was fitted and holds: the value at which each substrate transitions is
offset by variant and ordered by spectral gap, with the connectome lowest on the
median.


% P5: the free run, both halves of the pre-registration. Claims A3P.7, A3P.8.
% Figure F17. Numerals: 9, ALL act-file-only: TIER0 has no section for E2.
Everything so far is measured under teacher forcing, on a network being corrected
at every step. The final test releases it. A pre-registration written before the
code existed made two claims about what a free-running rollout would show: that
the connectome would retain the true attractor's statistics to a higher sign
fraction than the nulls, and that when substrates failed they would fail by
distorting the attractor's shape while preserving its scale. The first is
confirmed. Once a third of the weights are negative, the connectome holds a
faithful climate in
$0.43$ % A3P 6.1
of cells against the nulls'
$0.14$, % A3P 6.1
about three times the rate (Figure~\ref{fig:F17}). The second is refuted, and the
refutation is the more informative result. The ratio of the free run's spread to
the true trajectory's spread is bimodal:
$229$ % A3P 6.1
of
$440$ % A3P 6.1
cells fall below a twentieth, and
$158$ % A3P 6.1
sit within a tenth of unity, with
$11$ % A3P 6.1
cells anywhere in between. A failed rollout is a collapse of scale and not a
distortion of shape, and the registered description, wrong shape at right scale,
fits
$34$ % A3P 6.1
of the four hundred and forty. Nor do the collapsed rollouts settle at the origin:
most rest more than three z-scored units away, against a true attractor spanning
roughly two and a half units per coordinate, which is why their climate errors run
so high. This is the fixed-point branch of the map that opened this section,
observed in the closed loop. Two order parameters, one measured under drive and
one under autonomous rollout, on different quantities and different captures, are
both two-valued, and neither is graded.

% P1: one structural cause, two readouts with opposite preference. Numerals: 2.
The two results of this chapter were argued separately, on different tasks and at
different operating points, and they look like separate findings. They are not.
Each unit in these networks is a saturating map of its own recurrent input, and
such a map settles either onto a fixed point or onto a period-two orbit with
nothing stable between the two, so which branch a network occupies is the whole
of its dynamical state. A non-negative matrix is pinned to the first branch. Its
leading eigenvalue is positive by Perron--Frobenius, and because the diagonal is
zero the negative eigenvalues that must exist are bounded well below it, at
roughly
$0.33$ % TIER0 3.9
of the leading value for the connectome against Erdős--Rényi's
$0.55$. % TIER0 3.9
Making weights negative destroys that guarantee and brings the second branch
within reach. The consequence is that one structural property is read by the two
demands with opposite sign. A network sitting on the fixed-point branch produces
smooth trajectories, which is what a closed loop needs to keep generating; the
same settling synchronises the units, so they stop doing different things and the
readout dimensions a linear map could have used are wasted. What helps generation
is what costs memory. The two advantages could not have occupied the same region.

% P2: the two boundaries, and the crossing. Claim A3P.6. Figure F16.
% Numerals: 9.

the bulk radius fixed, the sign fraction at which the connectome's memory
advantage falls away rises from
$0$ to $0.19$, % TIER0 2.3
while the sign fraction at which its generative advantage sets in falls from
$0.35$ to $0.05$ % TIER0 2.3
(Figure~\ref{fig:F16}). One boundary climbs and the other descends, the gap
between them passes through zero once, and the two claims that
Section~\ref{sec:act3-memory-sigma} and
Section~\ref{sec:act3-lesion-generation} make in apparent contradiction are the
two sides of that gap. The crossing sits at
$\sigma \cdot \mathrm{bulk95} = 2.938$, $f = 0.153$, % TIER0 2.3
and it is the first crossing lying inside full replicate coverage. That
qualification is not decoration. Beyond the coverage edge the boundaries rest on
a subsample selected by bulk radius, further crossings appear there, and neither
their number nor their position may be read; what is defensible is that the
published crossing is first by a clear margin, the next lying more than half a
unit further out, and that it falls inside coverage under both of the conventions
by which coverage can be defined. The contour level that produces the boundaries
is likewise not free. Taken over the whole panel the largest generative effect is
$89$ times % TIER0 2.3
the largest one backed by every replicate, and it comes from a single cell backed

% P3: the bridge. Movement 3, ENTIRELY act-file-only, no figure. Numerals: 8.

What makes this an axis rather than a pair of tasks is a third task lying between
them. NARMA-10 asks a network to reproduce a nonlinear function of an input's
recent history, which needs both retention and a nonlinear transformation of it,
and its advantage over Erdős--Rényi peaks at an operating point between the other
two. At a quarter of weights negative, the connectome's advantage is largest at
$\sigma = 6$ % A3P 5.7
on memory, at
$\sigma = 4$ % A3P 5.7
on NARMA-10, and at
$\sigma = 2$ % A3P 5.7
on Lorenz, and the same ordering holds when the location is taken across the
whole range of sign fractions. Along the operating point, then, the three tasks
sit in the order their demands suggest. Along the sign fraction they do not.
NARMA-10's advantage is supercritical and largest where signs are least balanced,
$+0.12$ to $+0.23$ % A3P 5.7
in normalised error below a sign fraction of
$0.15$ % A3P 5.7
and decaying after it, which is memory's pattern and the opposite of the Lorenz
task's, whose advantage emerges only past
$0.20$. % A3P 5.7
The reason is mechanical rather than a matter of degree. NARMA-10 is teacher
forced, so the network is re-anchored to the true input at every step and never
runs on its own output; the regime it settles into has no chance to compound.
Whatever else divides these tasks, the division that matters here is not a
spectrum from remembering to predicting but the presence or absence of a closed
loop. Mackey-Glass, the fourth task, is absent from this comparison by a
scheduling decision rather than a gap in the argument.

% P4: what is measured and what is inferred, in the section's own voice.
% Numerals: 0.
It is worth being explicit about the standing of the account just given, because
the preceding paragraphs are the most unifying prose in this thesis. What is
measured is the two-state distribution of trajectory curvature, the fact that a
binary regime variable explains almost as much of prediction as the full geometry
does, the ordering of common-mode amplitude across substrates, the operating
points at which free-running trajectories collapse, and the two advantage regions
with the crossing between them. What is inference is the claim that a single
account, built on where a substrate's leading eigenvalue sits relative to the rest
of its spectrum, generates all of it. That account is consistent with everything
measured here and it is not a derivation: nothing in this work shows that the two
readouts must follow from the one cause, only that they do, wherever this work has
looked. It also has a boundary that is not a detail. It does not explain the
collapse observed with non-negative weights, where the same failure occurs with no
sign change to make the second branch reachable and with the geometry unmoved.
Section~\ref{sec:act3-scope} returns to it.

% P5: what the unification does not license. Numerals: 4.
Three limits bound the claim itself. It is a joint reading of two results and has
no out-of-sample test: no third substrate or third condition was held back and
predicted from it, so the unification describes what was found rather than
forecasting what would be. The crossing is a point where two estimated contours
meet on a plane, not an event anyone observed a network undergo, and it was never
a sharp feature even on the axis where it was first read, moving over
$3.85$ to $5.31$ % TIER0 2.3
in operating point under replicate resampling and failing to appear at all in
$29\%$ % TIER0 2.3
of resamples. And neither matching axis is neutral. The plane drawn here holds the
bulk radius fixed and leaves the Perron root unmatched, which hands the connectome
more of the property under test, while the nominal axis fixes the Perron root and
lets the bulk vary; the crossing exists on the first and not on the second. What
survives both readings is the shape rather than the coordinate: the two advantages
lie in opposite regions, and one substrate property, read by two demands, is what
puts them there.
