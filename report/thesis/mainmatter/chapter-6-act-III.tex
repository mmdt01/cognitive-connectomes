% P1: the two demands, and the mapping fixed in advance. Numerals: 0.
Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-II} measured what a driven network's activity looks
like. This chapter asks what that activity is worth. Two demands can be made of
it and they are not the same demand. One is to reconstruct an input the network
has already seen, which requires that traces of the past still be separable in
the present state. The other is to sustain a signal with no input arriving to
correct it, the network driving itself from its own output. The first asks how
many independent directions survive; the second asks whether the dynamics stay
in a regime where a trajectory can be continued at all. Both are put to the same
four substrates along the same axis, and the question the chapter closes on is
whether the two answers are two facts or one.

% P2: the mapping, declared before inspection. Numerals: 0.
Which task carries which demand was fixed before any of the data below was
inspected, so that no claim here can be rescued by the observation that there is
always some task on which it holds. Retention is carried by the
\textbf{memory-capacity} task, and the mapping places NARMA-10 and Mackey-Glass
on the same side of the dissociation rather than on the generative one.
Generation is carried by the \textbf{Lorenz prediction} task and has \emph{no}
corroborating task: the resistance margin and the regime switch reported below
rest on Lorenz alone. What the joint question costs, and what became of the
fourth task, is Section~\ref{sec:act3-scope}'s.

% P3: the primary variable and both matching axes, carried forward not derived.
% Sources: sheet 13, movement 3, rows 2, 3, 4. Numerals: 3.
Both demands are made along one variable. Every substrate here is run at a
nominal spectral radius $\sigma$, and that $\sigma$ is the \textbf{operating
point}: how hard the recurrent dynamics are driven. It is the primary variable
of this thesis, and every headline claim below is made along it.
Two matching axes follow from the normalisation, and
Chapter~\ref{chapter:methods} establishes both (Figure~\ref{fig:F3}). Matching
on nominal $\sigma$ holds the spectral radius fixed and lets the bulk radius
vary; matching on $\sigma\!\cdot\!\mathrm{bulk95}$ holds the bulk radius fixed
and leaves the Perron root deliberately unmatched. 
Neither choice is neutral, and the two are not neutral in the same direction.
The mechanism under test is the Perron mode, and the matched-bulk axis is the
one that leaves it unmatched: at the matched point the connectome runs at
$\sigma = 6.0$ against Erdős--Rényi's % TIER0 1.1
$3.54$, % TIER0 1.1
a Perron root $1.7$ times larger. % TIER0 1.1
That axis hands the connectome more of the very quantity proposed as the cause,
so an advantage measured on it cannot be an artifact of the matching. The
nominal axis is partial in the other direction. Neither is the chapter's axis:
each result below names the one it was measured on, and rests on surviving both.

Sections~\ref{sec:act3-memory-sigma} and~\ref{sec:act3-prediction-sigma} are
measured on non-negative weights throughout, which is the recurrent connectivity
the human structural connectome supplies. A manipulation arrives in
Section~\ref{sec:act3-lesion-memory}: the sign fraction $f$, the proportion of
weights made negative. It enters as an intervention on a substrate and not as a
second axis of the chapter. Macroscale diffusion tractography cannot represent
the sign of a connection, so $f = 0$ is what the instrument produces rather than
a property of the cortex, and grading $f$ upward asks what a network carrying
inhibition would do. A streamline count is non-negative by construction, while
the quantity it stands in for in these dynamics is a coupling, by which one
parcel's activity raises or lowers another's. The second statement concerns how
the results below are to be read against one another. They are measured at
operating points that differ by more than a factor of three, and the operating
point is not a detail of the setup but the variable the chapter is about: two of
the findings that follow, quoted without it, make each other look false. Every
claim below therefore names the operating point it was measured at and the axis
it was matched on, and none should be read without them.

% P2: why the peak cannot carry a claim. Claim A3M.2. Numerals: 1.
That the peak cannot carry a claim is not a limitation discovered afterwards.
Measured as the ridge effective rank (Equation~\ref{eq:deff}), every substrate
on the ladder peaks within a few percent of the hard
$d_{\mathrm{eff}} = N$ ceiling, % TIER0 1.2
because at its own optimum each is using very nearly every direction available
to it, and the larger network reported below does not escape the ceiling
either. Peak capacity is therefore not resolvable at this size, and no claim is
made about it in either direction. What is resolvable is what happens on either
side of the peak: a ceiling can clip curves but it cannot manufacture a
crossing, so a difference in how fast substrates fall away from their optima is
robust to finite size in a way a difference between the optima is not. This
section therefore focuses on reading the decay region.

% P3: the crossing survives the axis, and what it is not. Numerals: 0.
% The nominal-axis statement is TIER0 1.2's prose. NO figure reference: see the
% verification note at the head of this section.
The crossing is not a property of the axis that produces it. The same retention
ordering holds on the nominal axis, where the Perron root rather than the bulk
is what is held fixed, % TIER0 1.2
so it survives the matching choice that hands the connectome the larger
spectral gap.
What it is not is a global advantage. The ladder ordering is regional: it runs
the other way below criticality, where the nominal axis leaves the bulk
unmatched, it is absent near the peak, and it is strong and consistent above
criticality. Stated on the axis that holds the bulk fixed, where the subcritical
deficit is not present at all, the defensible summary is parity below
criticality and advantage above it.

% P4: parity at the peak, with its interval. Claims A3M.3, A3M.7. Figure F10.

The peaks cannot be separated as capacities, but they can be compared as a
statement about their similarity, and that comparison is worth making precisely
because it runs against the chapter's direction. Paired within seed, each
substrate at its own optimum and each pair sharing input weights and input
series, the connectome's peak memory capacity sits
$2$ to $6\%$ % TIER0 3.4
below the nulls' (Figure~\ref{fig:F10}). The intervals are what make that
readable. Against Erdős--Rényi and against the weight-permuted placement control
the difference excludes zero at every ridge strength tested; against
degree-matching it does so at
one of five, % TIER0 3.4
and at one of them the point estimate changes sign. The defensible statement is
therefore \emph{parity} at the peak, not a deficit, and certainly not that this
substrate is the worst of the four. That the five ridge strengths span five
orders of magnitude makes the panel a robustness check rather than a search:
usable dimensionality tracks measured memory at every one of them, provided the
regularisation is raised in both places at once, so the readout's regularisation
is not what produces the result in either direction.

% P7: the mechanism, using chapter 5 and not re-establishing it. Claim A3M.5.
% Numerals: 5. F11(a) is forward-referenced, not floated: open flag 3.
What the connectome's unique spectral gap buys is a common mode that can be driven without the rest
of the spectrum going with it. A non-negative matrix has an all-positive leading
eigenvector by Perron--Frobenius, so every substrate here carries a common mode,
and driving the network harder drives that mode harder. The nulls give way to it.
At the supercritical operating point $\sigma = 6$ their common-mode amplitude stands at
$0.949$ to $0.989$, % TIER0 3.7
which is very nearly every unit pinned at the same saturated value, doing the
same thing at the same time. The connectome reaches
$0.759$, % TIER0 3.7
the least dominated of the four, and it does so while carrying by far the
largest Perron root: the substrate with the most to be dominated by is the one
least dominated by it. That is what a large spectral gap does. It lets the
leading mode be driven hard without the bulk of the spectrum following it, so
the activity that is not the common mode survives, and what survives clear of
the readout's regularisation floor is what the readout can use. Supercritically
the connectome holds
$89.0\%$ of its directions more than a decade clear of that floor against
Erdős--Rényi's $11.4\%$ % TIER0 3.6
(Section~\ref{sec:act2-floor}). The decay difference in Figure~\ref{fig:F7} is
that gap, read out. Figure~\ref{fig:F11}a draws the amplitudes across all four
substrates, in Section~\ref{sec:act3-lesion-memory}, where the axis it is drawn
against is the argument.

% P8: the finding as range, then both of its costs. Numerals: 0.
The finding is about range rather than height. Every substrate here has an
operating point at which it does its best work, and at that point they are hard
to tell apart; move away from it and they part company, the connectome carrying
most of its memory with it while the nulls leave most of theirs behind. Across
the shared range the spread in what survives is four-fold, and the substrate
that retains most is the one that peaked lowest. So the quantity that separates
these substrates is not how much memory a network has at its best but how much
of it is still there once the network is driven somewhere else. The mechanism is
a rescue rather than a gain: non-negativity costs every substrate memory, and
the connectome's spectral gap makes it cost least. Both halves of that sentence
carry. At its own optimum this substrate is not ahead, and the interval says so. 
On the quantity the reservoir literature has largely optimised, peak capacity at minimal regularisation, it
loses, and the ordering that says so is reproduced above rather than explained
away. Neither cost touches the finding, because neither is about the same
quantity: a peak is a height and this result is a width. What the account still
lacks is a test rather than an illustration. Every substrate compared so far
carries a common mode, because every one of them has non-negative weights, so
comparison alone cannot show that the common mode is what the difference is made
of. The removal comes in Section~\ref{sec:act3-lesion-memory}. Before that, the 
the same axis has a second demand to answer first, and prediction cannot be scored by looking at the past at all.

Unlike a network scored on memory, which is handed the true signal at every step, 
a network asked to generate is released after a short
initialisation and runs on its own output, so its mistakes become its own next
input and accumulate. What is scored is how long it stays with the true signal
before leaving it. Asked that way, and asked where each substrate is at its best,
near criticality at $\sigma = 2$, the four are not distinguishable: the
connectome's margins over the three nulls are
$+0.28$, $-0.01$ and $+0.44$ % TIER0 2.6
Lyapunov times, none of them significant. That is the same shape as the memory
result from the previous section, where the four peaks also sat within a few percent of
each other. Once again the substrates separate away from their best operating
points rather than at them.

% P2: the failure the connectome never has. Claim A3P.4. Numerals: 3.
Driven well past that point, the nulls stop predicting altogether. Above
$\sigma \approx 7.6$ to $8.0$, % TIER0 2.3
five of Erdős--Rényi's ten seeds settle onto a pair of states and alternate
between them, flipping from one to the other at every step and going nowhere
else. What they generate is no longer a path that winds around the Lorenz
attractor; it is two points. Across the whole swept range, to more than five
times the radius at which its own bulk edge reaches one, this happens to none of
the connectome's ten seeds. The substrates are not being separated here by how
long they predict for, but by whether what they produce is a trajectory at all.
Five against none is significant
($p = 0.033$, % TIER0 2.3
Fisher exact), though on ten seeds per substrate the claim rests on the size and
direction of the difference rather than on the test.

% P3: what accounts for it, and where it is earned. Numerals: 0.
% Names the mechanism WITHOUT claiming it here. 6.6 earns it.
What the connectome brings to that comparison is the same spectral gap the
memory result turned on, read from its other end. Alternation between two states
is what a network settles into when the most negative part of its spectrum grows
strong enough to dominate its own dynamics, and a large gap holds that part far
below the leading eigenvalue. The substrate whose spectrum is most spread out
between its extremes is therefore the one furthest from reaching the alternating
state, and it stays furthest from it however hard it is driven. Stated here, that
is an account which fits; it is not yet one that has been tested, because every
substrate compared so far has the same sign structure and so none of them can
isolate it. Section~\ref{sec:act3-unifying} returns to it once an intervention
has made the test possible.

% P4: the bound on the other side. Numerals: 0.
% Negative result kept beside the claim it bounds (CONVENTIONS working rule 8).
% The V4 correction: the separation is SHOWN, not inferred from absence.
Resisting that particular failure does not make this substrate reliable. On a
blunter measure, counting how many seeds stop predicting at all somewhere high in
the sweep, the ordering reverses and the connectome is the worst of the four,
while the degree-matched null never fails at any operating point. The two
failures are separate events rather than degrees of one, and can be shown to be:
no seed of the connectome that stops predicting is a seed that has fallen into
the alternating state, and it has none of the latter anywhere.
Appendix~\ref{app:second-failure} gives the counts. What holds is the narrower
statement, that the connectome resists one specific way of failing to generate.

Set against how much prediction is lost across the sweep, none of this shows up
in the activity itself. The connectome's curvature, the mean angle between
successive segments of its trajectory, holds at
$0.26$~rad % TIER0 3.11
from just above criticality to the top of the sweep, while over the same range
its prediction falls roughly tenfold (Figure~\ref{fig:F21}). The measure that
carried the memory half of this chapter registers nothing at all here: capacity
is lost with the geometry intact, and what governs that loss is left open, having
resisted the explanations this thesis could test. Something can still be done
about the question, and it is not another comparison. Every substrate to this
point has non-negative weights, so every one of them carries a common mode, and
no contrast between them can show that the common mode is what any of these
differences are made of. The next section stops comparing and starts changing the
weights.

% P1: why an intervention is needed, and what this one is. Numerals: 0.
The comparison of Section~\ref{sec:act3-memory-sigma} cannot settle its own
mechanism. Every substrate on the ladder is non-negative, so by
Perron--Frobenius every one of them has an all-positive leading eigenvector and
therefore a common mode, and a comparison between four substrates that all carry
the same feature cannot establish that the feature is what separates them. The
account offered there was that a large spectral gap lets the leading mode be
driven hard without the bulk of the spectrum following it. If that is right, then
taking the common mode away should take the difference with it. This section does
exactly that. A fraction $f$ of the weights is made negative, leaving the
magnitudes untouched and the topology intact, so that what changes between one
network and the next is the sign pattern and nothing else. As $f$ approaches one
half the excitatory and inhibitory contributions to each unit's input come into
balance and the all-positive leading direction has nothing left to be built from.
The manipulation is not a perturbation away from biology. Macroscale diffusion
tractography cannot represent the sign of a connection, so a non-negative matrix
is what the instrument produces rather than what cortex is, and increasing $f$
restores the half of the biology the measurement cannot see. It enters here as an
intervention on a substrate, not as a second axis of the chapter: every operating
point in this section is held fixed. 
\footnote{Which edges are inverted is controlled rather than left to chance.
Edges are ranked by how central their two endpoints are in the network and
divided into ten equal groups, and the same proportion is inverted at random
within each, so that the inverted set has the same hub-to-periphery composition
as the graph at every $f$. The alternative, drawing uniformly from all edges,
would let the amount of sign and its placement vary together: inverting an edge
between two hubs disturbs the leading eigenvector far more than inverting a
peripheral one, so at a fixed $f$ the spectral effect would depend on how many
hub edges the draw happened to catch. Chapter~\ref{chapter:methods} gives the
procedure in full.}

Section~\ref{sec:act3-lesion-memory} removed the common mode and the memory
ladder dissolved with it. The same intervention applied to generation does
something else entirely, and seeing why requires one fact about what a negative
weight does dynamically. Each unit in the network is a saturating map of its own
recurrent input: it takes the weighted sum reaching it, adds the drive, and
passes the result through a $\tanh$. Considered on its own, such a map has a
stable fixed point when its effective gain is sufficiently positive and a stable
period-two orbit when the gain is sufficiently negative, and between those two
regimes it has no stable behaviour at all. With non-negative weights only the
first branch is reachable. Making a fraction of the weights negative opens the
second. The consequence for this section is that the intervention does not turn a
dial: it makes a second dynamical regime available, and a network either enters it
or does not. Everything below follows from that, and the first thing to check is
whether the geometry behaves as the map says it should.

such a map settles either onto a fixed point or onto a period-two orbit with

same settling synchronises the units, so they stop doing different things and the
readout dimensions a linear map could have used are wasted. What helps generation
is what costs memory. The two advantages could not have occupied the same region.

What makes this an axis rather than a pair of tasks is a third task lying between
them. NARMA-10 asks a network to reproduce a nonlinear function of an input's
recent history, which needs both retention and a nonlinear transformation of it,
and its advantage over Erdős--Rényi peaks at an operating point between the other
two. At a quarter of weights negative, the connectome's advantage is largest at
$\sigma = 6$ % A3P 5.7
on memory, at
$\sigma = 4$ % A3P 5.7
on NARMA-10, and at
$\sigma = 2$ % A3P 5.7
on Lorenz, and the same ordering holds when the location is taken across the
whole range of sign fractions. Along the operating point, then, the three tasks
sit in the order their demands suggest. Along the sign fraction they do not.
NARMA-10's advantage is supercritical and largest where signs are least balanced,
$+0.12$ to $+0.23$ % A3P 5.7
in normalised error below a sign fraction of
$0.15$ % A3P 5.7
and decaying after it, which is memory's pattern and the opposite of the Lorenz
task's, whose advantage emerges only past
$0.20$. % A3P 5.7
The reason is mechanical rather than a matter of degree. NARMA-10 is teacher
forced, so the network is re-anchored to the true input at every step and never
runs on its own output; the regime it settles into has no chance to compound.
Whatever else divides these tasks, the division that matters here is not a
spectrum from remembering to predicting but the presence or absence of a closed
loop. Mackey-Glass, the fourth task, is absent from this comparison by a
scheduling decision rather than a gap in the argument.

% P4: what is measured and what is inferred, in the section's own voice.
% Numerals: 0.
It is worth being explicit about the standing of the account just given, because
the preceding paragraphs are the most unifying prose in this thesis. What is
measured is the two-state distribution of trajectory curvature, the fact that a
binary regime variable explains almost as much of prediction as the full geometry
does, the ordering of common-mode amplitude across substrates, the operating
points at which free-running trajectories collapse, and the two advantage regions
with the crossing between them. What is inference is the claim that a single
account, built on where a substrate's leading eigenvalue sits relative to the rest
of its spectrum, generates all of it. That account is consistent with everything
measured here and it is not a derivation: nothing in this work shows that the two
readouts must follow from the one cause, only that they do, wherever this work has
looked. It also has a boundary that is not a detail. It does not explain the
collapse observed with non-negative weights, where the same failure occurs with no
sign change to make the second branch reachable and with the geometry unmoved.
Section~\ref{sec:act3-scope} returns to it.

% P5: what the unification does not license. Numerals: 4.
Three limits bound the claim itself. It is a joint reading of two results and has
no out-of-sample test: no third substrate or third condition was held back and
predicted from it, so the unification describes what was found rather than
forecasting what would be. The crossing is a point where two estimated contours
meet on a plane, not an event anyone observed a network undergo, and it was never
a sharp feature even on the axis where it was first read, moving over
$3.85$ to $5.31$ % TIER0 2.3
in operating point under replicate resampling and failing to appear at all in
$29\%$ % TIER0 2.3
of resamples. And neither matching axis is neutral. The plane drawn here holds the
bulk radius fixed and leaves the Perron root unmatched, which hands the connectome
more of the property under test, while the nominal axis fixes the Perron root and
lets the bulk vary; the crossing exists on the first and not on the second. What
survives both readings is the shape rather than the coordinate: the two advantages
lie in opposite regions, and one substrate property, read by two demands, is what
puts them there.

% P1: the account is a statement about the f > 0 counterfactual. Numerals: 6.
The account assembled over the preceding sections has a boundary, and it runs
through the middle of this chapter rather than around its edge. Capacity is gated
by which dynamical regime the manifold occupies: that is a statement about
networks with negative weights, and it is not a statement about the substrate the
instrument actually produces. With non-negative weights there is no switch to be
gated by. Trajectory curvature holds at
$0.26$~rad % TIER0 3.11
across the entire operating-point sweep while prediction falls roughly ten-fold,
so generation is lost there with no geometric event of any kind, and the quantity
the mechanism is built on registers nothing. This is consistent with the account
rather than a contradiction of it, which is exactly why it bounds it: the switch
is a phenomenon of sign composition, and a non-negative matrix has no dominant
negative eigenvalue to flip. Take the negative weights away and the switch goes,
and the explanation resting on it goes with it. What replaces it is a named open
question rather than a fourth guess. Section~\ref{sec:act3-prediction-sigma}
eliminated the three available answers: not geometry, which does not move; not the
effective radius, since Erdős--Rényi transitions at
$0.014$, % TIER0 3.10
two orders of magnitude below its own largest value and on the descending branch
of its own curve; and not memory, since at $\sigma = 6$ the connectome holds
$11.43$ against Erdős--Rényi's $2.42$ % TIER0 3.11
in memory capacity and a lower valid prediction time,
$0.81$ against $1.18$. % TIER0 3.11
Whatever governs generation on non-negative weights, this work has not found it,
and no part of the unifying account should be read as covering it.

% P2: contribution 2 has no out-of-sample test. Numerals: 0.
% Must not imply MG fills the gap; must not say MG was analysed or not collected.
The unifying claim has a second limit, of a different kind. It rests on a joint
reading of the memory and Lorenz results and on the plane that puts them together,
and nothing was held back from it. The mapping of claims to the tasks that carry
them was fixed before any data was inspected, which is what prevents a claim being
relocated to whichever task happens to support it, and under that mapping the
joint claim's evidence is the two tasks it unifies and no third. So there is no
out-of-sample test of it in this thesis. The obvious candidate does not serve. The
fourth task in the programme, Mackey-Glass, is implemented in a teacher-forced
form, so the network is re-anchored to the true signal at every step and never
runs on its own output; the regime switch that the account turns on has no
consequence for what that task measures, which makes a prediction on it
uninterpretable rather than merely difficult to detect. That was recognised as a
design problem before any of its data was looked at, and the original registration
was withdrawn on those grounds and a narrower one recorded in its place, on the
memory side. A closed-loop version would be the test, and building it is deferred
work rather than something the task's presence in the programme quietly supplies.
No Mackey-Glass result is inspected anywhere in this thesis.

% P3: the limits on each arm's own evidence. Numerals: 4.
Three narrower limits attach to the results themselves. The memory comparison
cannot be made at the peak at any network size: usable dimensionality there runs
at
$0.97$ to $1.00$ % TIER0 6.6
of its ceiling even at the larger scale tested, so no parcellation makes that
comparison informative and the chapter reads the decay region instead. Whether the
bulk radius is what orders the ladder remains open and is only partly answered:
matching on it absorbs about
a quarter % TIER0 3.7
of the gap where a common mode exists and almost none once the signs are balanced,
which bounds the quantity's role without settling it, and a properly powered test
would need more seeds or a pair of substrates whose separation in that quantity is
both large and stable across scales. And the generative result at zero sign
fraction rests on
ten seeds % TIER0 2.3
per substrate. With that sample the smallest attainable significance is bounded
from below whatever the effect, so the family of comparisons it belongs to is
declared narrowly and the claim rests on the size and direction of the asymmetry
rather than on the test alone.

% P4: what the matched axis does and does not match, and reproducibility.
% Numerals: 2. Closes the chapter.
Two limits are properties of the instruments rather than of the results. The
matched axis used throughout holds a property of the linear operator fixed, and
that is not the same as holding the dynamics fixed: at the point where each
substrate's effective radius folds, the gain the units are actually running at
differs across substrates, at
$0.54$ for the connectome against $0.69$ % TIER0 6.2
for Erdős--Rényi. That difference is part of the mechanism rather than a nuisance
to be matched away, so matched effective criticality must be read narrowly as
what it is: two networks placed at the same value of one spectral quantity, not
two networks in the same dynamical state. The second is reproducibility. Which
edges receive negative signs depends on a sort over a heavily tied ordering, so
the pattern is exactly reproducible on the machine that produced a given capture
and not across machines. Every distribution reported here was checked and agrees,
but individual cell values at non-zero sign fraction are not portable, and any
reanalysis should work from the distributions rather than expecting to reproduce
particular cells. None of this is offered as a reason to discount the chapter's
results. It is offered because the account is worth exactly what it covers, and
the reader should not have to work out where that ends.
