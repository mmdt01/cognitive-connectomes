\section{The substrate and the null ladder}
\label{sec:act1-substrate}
\bigskip
% Outline item 2. No results: every numeral below is a design fact or a count.
% Construction, generation procedures and null validation are in Chapter 3.

% P1: the substrate, and the two properties everything downstream rests on.
The substrate is a human structural connectome at 448 cortical
parcels, % METH A.4
built for this thesis as a group consensus over the diffusion-weighted scans of
a publicly released healthy-adult cohort \cite{griffa2019structural,
suarez2021learning} rather than taken from a published matrix. Two of its
properties carry through the whole thesis. It is symmetric, which
Section~\ref{sec:act1-spectrum-real} takes up immediately. And it is
non-negative throughout, because tractography counts streamlines and cannot
represent an inhibitory connection, so a macro-scale structural connectome has
no way to express one. The same build at
$N = 1000$ % METH A.4
supports the change of scale in Section~\ref{sec:act1-scale}. Construction is in
Chapter~\ref{chapter:methods}.

% P2: the three randomised substrates the figures draw.
Against the connectome stand three randomised substrates, each destroying some
part of its organisation and holding the rest fixed.
Table~\ref{tab:act1-preservation} sets out what each keeps, and
Figure~\ref{fig:F19} shows what that looks like as a graph. An Erdős–Rényi
graph matches the node count, edge count and density exactly rather than in
expectation, and panel~(d) is the result: connections scattered uniformly, with
none of the block structure of panel~(a). A degree-preserving rewire adds the
degree sequence, so it carries the connectome's heavy-tailed degree
distribution intact, visible in the marginal above panel~(c), while the blocks
go the same way. Weights are painted onto both by drawing, with replacement,
from the connectome's own multiset of edge
weights, % METH B.0
so the ladder varies where weight sits without changing what weights are
available.

% P3: the control, which is the chapter's instrument.
The third randomised substrate is not a null but a control. The weight-permuted variant
takes the connectome's own graph and its own weights and permutes the weights
across the edges, and panels~(a) and~(b) of Figure~\ref{fig:F19} are the same
image because of it: same edge set, same degrees, and the same weight multiset
exactly rather than in distribution, since the values are reordered rather than
resampled. % METH B.1
The magnified block below makes the one difference legible. In (i) and (j) the
same cells are filled and the colours in them are not the same colours. No
measure of the binary graph separates the two substrates, which is what
Table~\ref{tab:act1-topology} reports: identical clustering, identical
modularity on a fixed partition, identical assortativity and identical
efficiency, as any such measure must return on one graph. That is what makes
the control the instrument this chapter turns on the question. Against the
connectome it isolates weight placement, holding topology and weight statistics
fixed together; against the degree-preserving rewire it isolates
topology, since the two draw their weights from the same pool and differ in the
graph. Each of the three randomised substrates is generated at ten
seeds; % METH B.0
the connectome is one fixed graph and returns the same matrix every time. Three
further substrates enter no figure in this chapter and are described in
Chapter~\ref{chapter:methods}; Figure~\ref{fig:S4} draws the whole family
together and their spectra are in Appendix~\ref{app:wider-ladder}.

\section{The spectrum is real, and it has one outlier}
\label{sec:act1-spectrum-real}
\bigskip
% Outline item 3. Carries A1.1. Figure F1a.
% The bulk95 definition and the percentile-population caveat are in Chapter 3.

% P1: what the spectrum looks like, and why the figure takes the form it does.
The connectome's spectrum is a dense band about the origin and one eigenvalue
standing well clear of it at the right-hand edge, which is panel~(a) of
Figure~\ref{fig:F1}. It is also entirely real. The consensus build makes the
substrate symmetric, and a symmetric matrix is normal, so its eigenvalues are
real. This is not an approximation that holds well enough to work with: across
all forty ladder cells at $N = 448$ the largest imaginary part in the stored
spectra is exactly zero, % A1 2.5
and the symmetry check holds in every row. % A1 2.5
It is also why Figure~\ref{fig:F1} is a density on the real line rather than a
cloud in the complex plane, where every point would lie on a single axis and the
second dimension would carry nothing.

% P2: why the outlier is guaranteed, and the vocabulary the rest of the thesis uses.
The separation is not an accident of this particular graph, and non-negativity
is why. The Perron--Frobenius theorem guarantees a non-negative matrix a real,
positive leading eigenvalue, largest in modulus, whose eigenvector is
non-negative throughout; the only question a given substrate answers is how far
clear of everything else that eigenvalue sits. Three terms follow from the
picture and are used for the rest of this thesis. The \emph{Perron root}
$\lambda_1$ is that leading eigenvalue, the \emph{bulk} is the band holding the
remainder, and the \emph{gap} is the separation between them. Measuring the gap
needs an edge for the bulk, and the one used throughout is
$\mathrm{bulk95}$, the 95th percentile of $|\lambda|$ over the spectrum as a
fraction of $\lambda_1$. % A1 2.6 finding 1
All three are properties of a single substrate, measured before any comparison
is made.

\section{The bulk is everyone's, the gap is not, and it is placement}
\label{sec:act1-bulk-gap-placement}
\bigskip
% Outline items 4 and 5, merged per act1_structure.md section 5 item 16.
% Carries A1.2, A1.3, A1.5. Figures F1, F2.

% P1: the headline statistic, first sentence. Carries A1.3.
The connectome's Perron root stands
more than three % (3.078) TIER0 3.1
times clear of its own bulk edge at $N = 448$. % TIER0 3.1
Every other substrate the figures draw stands just below two
% between 1.807 (TIER0 3.1) and 1.922 (TIER0 3.1)
times clear of its own. That ratio, the Perron root over the absolute bulk
radius, is the structural statistic this chapter reports, and the connectome's
is at least 1.6-fold % TIER0 3.1(b)
above every rung of the four-substrate ladder it is measured against. The gap is
not bought by a narrower bulk. It is bought by a higher outlier over a bulk that
is essentially everyone's.

% P2: the decomposition, in raw units. Carries A1.2.
Figure~\ref{fig:F1} draws the eigenvalues of the un-rescaled matrix $W$, one
substrate per row, on a shared axis in the raw units of the weights, and the
first thing to do with it is to sight down the column. The four bulk bands sit
at the same place. Their edges fall between
0.0587 % TIER0 3.1
and 0.0614, % TIER0 3.1
a spread of 4.4\% % TIER0 3.1
of their mean at $N = 448$, % TIER0 3.1
taking a spread throughout as the range over the mean. % A1 5.2
What moves is the outlier. The Perron root runs from
0.1061 % TIER0 3.1
at the Erdős–Rényi rung to 0.1889 % TIER0 3.1
at the connectome, so the grey region separating the bulk from $\lambda_1$ grows
steadily up the column while the coloured band beneath it stays put.
Figure~\ref{fig:F2} makes the same decomposition as medians across the ladder,
the absolute bulk in panel~(a) and the Perron root in panel~(b), and
Table~\ref{tab:act1-ladder} gives the values at both parcellations.

% P3: what the normalisation manufactures. Carries A1.2, second half.
Reported instead as a fraction of each substrate's own Perron root, those same
four bulk radii spread 47.3\%. % TIER0 3.1
Nothing about the bulks has moved between those two numbers. Only the divisor
has. Panel~(e) is where that divisor becomes visible: it redraws the same
spectra as cumulative distributions after each substrate has been divided by its
own $|\lambda_1|$, the normalisation every spectral-radius-matched comparison in
the connectome-reservoir literature
uses \cite{suarez2021learning, suarez2024connectome, milisav2026neuromorphic}.
In those units the curves separate everywhere and the connectome's reads as the
narrow one, with $\mathrm{bulk95}$ legible at the crossings of the 95\% level:
0.325 % A1 3, F1 caption
for the connectome against 0.52 to 0.55 % A1 3, F1 caption
for the nulls. The two halves of the figure are the same spectra one division
apart.

% P4: the retraction, stated once, in the direction the data supports.
That is what replaces an earlier reading of these same spectra, and the one the
literature invites: that biological weight placement produces an anomalously
compact bulk. It does not. What placement raises is the Perron root, by a factor
of 1.78 % TIER0 3.1
over the Erdős–Rényi rung at $N = 448$. % TIER0 3.1(a)
The compactness is manufactured by dividing by the one quantity that differs.
The finding is the same fact stated in the direction the data supports: an
anomalously large spectral gap.

% P5: the identity, and the name that carries forward. Carries A1.3.
The ratio has two other names, and all three are the same number:
$|\lambda_1| / \mathrm{abs\_bulk} = 1/\mathrm{bulk95} = \mathrm{sr\_crit}$. % TIER0 3.1
The gap ratio, the inverse bulk and the critical scale are one quantity, which
is why panel~(f) can carry $\mathrm{bulk95}$ on its right-hand axis, and why the
name $\mathrm{sr\_crit}$ costs nothing to adopt: it names the quantity after what
it measures, the nominal spectral radius at which the median seed's bulk edge
reaches one.\footnote{The edge in question is the 95th percentile of
$|\lambda|$, % A1 2.6 finding 1
so at $\sigma = \mathrm{sr\_crit}$ five per cent % A1 2.6 finding 1
of modes already exceed unity. $\mathrm{sr\_crit}$ is a criticality scale each
substrate carries with it, not the point at which that substrate becomes
supercritical. The identity holds under the product-of-medians aggregation used
throughout; taking the median of the per-seed products instead breaks it by up
to 0.055 at $N = 1000$. % A1 2.3
}

% P6: it is placement, and what that does not license. Carries A1.5.
What produces the gap is settled by the second row of the figure. The
weight-permuted control differs from the connectome in nothing but which edge
carries which weight, and it lands with the nulls, at a gap ratio of
1.922 % TIER0 3.1
against the connectome's 3.078. % TIER0 3.1
Neither the topology nor the weight distribution is sufficient on its own, and
the alignment between the two is what buys the gap. The limit is worth stating
rather than leaving to be inferred. The contrast localises the effect to
placement; it does not say which feature of the placement is responsible, and no
mechanism for why placement produces the gap is claimed here or anywhere in this
thesis.

% P6b: the pre-registered mechanism test, and what it ruled out.
One account was specific enough to test. In a weighted rich club the heavy
edges run between the high-degree nodes, so they share endpoints and reinforce
one another in the Perron root, and a weight permutation destroys exactly that
arrangement. The prediction was registered before the measurement and it
fails: edge weight and endpoint degree product correlate at
$-0.124$ % TIER0 3.14
in the connectome, negative rather than positive and with a bootstrap interval
excluding zero, while the control sits at zero as its construction
requires. % TIER0 3.14
The connectome's heavy edges run between its lower-degree parcels, not between
its hubs, so whatever placement is doing to the Perron root, it is not
assembling a heavily weighted core. Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism} gives the
test, its two other predictions, and the account left standing by it, which
this thesis does not test.

% P7: the wider ladder, as corroboration only. Full numbers in the appendix.
Two further rungs hold more of the connectome's topology fixed than the
degree-preserving rewire does, one preserving its community structure and one
its clustering, and neither recovers the gap; their gap ratios at both
parcellations, and the margin over all six non-negative substrates, are in
Appendix~\ref{app:wider-ladder}. Those rungs corroborate the claim. The claim
itself rests on the control, which holds the topology exactly rather than one
statistic of it at a time.

Panel~(a) is where the second half shows, and the two must be kept apart in
every sentence that mentions either. At $N = 448$ the four absolute bulk radii
agree to 4.4\% % TIER0 3.1
of their mean; at $N = 1000$ they spread 6.4\%, % TIER0 3.1

the larger parcellation, and its rows are in ladder order, so at $N = 1000$

\section{What carries forward: a critical scale and a mode}
\label{sec:act1-handover}
\bigskip
% Outline item 7. No new claims. One paragraph. No numerals by design.

Two things leave this chapter. The first is $\mathrm{sr\_crit}$, the nominal
spectral radius at which a substrate's own bulk edge reaches one. Because the
gap ratio is also the inverse bulk, every substrate arrives carrying its own,
and the four drawn here do not carry the same one. That is what makes a second
matching axis possible at all: alongside the nominal spectral radius, which
holds the Perron root fixed, the product $\sigma \cdot \mathrm{bulk95}$ holds
the bulk fixed instead. What this chapter supplies is the quantity that axis is
built from, and no more. It does not make the axis neutral, for the reason
Chapter~\ref{chapter:methods} gave: these substrates differ in one quantity
only, so each axis fixes exactly what the other lets vary. The second thing
handed on is the Perron mode itself. This chapter has established that it stands
clear of the bulk and that weight placement is what puts it there. It has
established nothing about what the mode does. A spectrum describes an operator,
and the question the next chapter asks is where each part of it ends up in the
activity that operator generates. The mode arrives there as an object to be
decomposed rather than as an explanation, and it arrives with that limit still
attached.

% One use of it is worth flagging now, since it is a choice rather than a
% consequence: the filter that separates supercritical from subcritical operating
% points in Chapters~\ref{chapter:act-II} and~\ref{chapter:act-III} is the
% connectome's own critical scale, applied to every substrate. It is the
% conservative option, because it samples each null further above its own critical
% point than the null's own scale would, and it is set out where it is used.
