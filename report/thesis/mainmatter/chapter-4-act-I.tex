\section{The substrate and the null ladder}
\label{sec:act1-substrate}
\bigskip
% Outline item 2. No results: every numeral below is a design fact, a count or a
% null validation. Sources: METHODS_SUBSTRATE.md (code), A1 section 5 (checks).

% P1: the substrate, the scales, and which modality is used.
The substrate is a group-consensus human structural connectome, built for this
thesis rather than taken from a published matrix.
The individual data are the 70 % METH A.1
diffusion-weighted datasets of a publicly released healthy-adult
cohort \cite{griffa2019structural}, in the form distributed with the
connectome-reservoir release of Suárez et al. \cite{suarez2021learning}.
The release provides the same cohort at several parcellation resolutions of the
Lausanne atlas, and at each resolution it provides a structural and a functional
connectome defined on the same anatomical parcels. This thesis works at the two
highest-resolution scales, $N = 448$ and $N = 1000$ (number of nodes in the network). % METH A.4
Both of the measures this chapter reports are percentiles taken over a spectrum,
and the reservoir measures in later chapters are ranks of an $N$-dimensional
state space, so each needs enough nodes to be informative; the coarser scales do
not supply them. Everything up to Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-III} is built on the
structural connectome alone, which is what instantiates the reservoir's
recurrent connectivity throughout. The functional connectomes are used only in
Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-IV}, and it is their being defined on the same parcels
as the structural matrix that makes that comparison possible at all.
Each individual structural matrix is restricted to cortical parcels, and its
edge weights are streamline fibre densities.
The group matrix is formed by a distance-dependent consensus
rule \cite{betzel2019distance}: candidate edges are grouped into bins by the
Euclidean distance between parcel centroids, with inter-hemispheric and
intra-hemispheric connections treated separately, and one edge is retained per
bin, the one present in the largest number of individuals. A retained edge takes
as its weight the mean of that connection across all subjects. The result is
symmetric, has a zero diagonal, and is non-negative throughout, since
tractography counts streamlines and cannot represent an inhibitory connection.
At $N = 448$ it holds
5{,}323 % METH A.6
undirected edges, a density of 5.32\%; % METH A.6
the same build at $N = 1000$ gives
10{,}784 % METH A.6
edges and 2.16\%. % METH A.6

% P2: the full null family, what each holds fixed, and which rungs carry the chapter.
Against the connectome stand five null models, each destroying some part of the connectome's
organisation and holding the rest fixed. In order of how much they keep: a
random gaussian matrix, which shares only the node count and whose weights are
signed; an Erdős–Rényi graph, matching the node count, edge count and density
exactly rather than in expectation; a degree-preserving rewire, which adds the
degree sequence and so carries the connectome's heavy-tailed degree
distribution intact; and clustering-preserving and modularity-preserving
rewires, each holding fixed everything the degree rewire does and one further
property of the topology besides.
Table~\ref{tab:act1-preservation} sets out what each rung preserves and what it
destroys; the generation procedures are given in
Chapter~\ref{chapter:methods}. Weights are painted onto every randomised graph
by drawing, with replacement, from the connectome's own multiset of edge
weights, % METH B.0
so the ladder varies where weight sits without changing what weights are
available. Each null is generated at ten seeds; % METH B.0
the connectome is one fixed graph and returns the same matrix every time.

% P3: the placement control.
In addition to the nulls, a control model of the connectome is used to investigate 
the effect that weight placement has on the connectome's topology.
The weight-permuted variant takes the connectome's own graph and its own weights and
permutes the weights across the edges: same edge set, same degrees, and the same
weight multiset exactly rather than in distribution, since the values are
reordered rather than resampled. % METH B.1
The only thing that differs from the connectome is which edge carries which
weight. That makes it the sharpest of the three comparisons available here.
Against the connectome it isolates weight placement, holding topology and weight
statistics fixed together; against the degree-preserving rewire it isolates
topology, since the two share a weight multiset and differ in the graph. Every
figure in this chapter carries it for that reason.

% P4: what the construction checks establish, and what the convergence evidence is.
Each rung asserts its own defining property at construction, and those
assertions are weaker than they look. All of them test a property the generator
preserves by construction, % METH B.6
so they will catch a mis-wired pipeline, a transposed mask or a generator called
on the wrong graph, and none of them can report whether a randomisation has
actually explored anything. The distinction matters for the degree-preserving
rewire, the one rung whose randomisation is iterative: its assertion holds after
any number of swaps, including none at all. That the chain has in fact mixed is
a separate measurement. At the production setting of ten accepted swaps per
edge, the rewired graph retains 3.6\% % A1 5.3
of the connectome's edges against a chance floor of roughly
2.2\% % A1 5.3
at this density, and retention is flat from five swaps per edge through to
forty, % A1 5.3
so the chain has settled well before the setting these results use.

% P5: Reasoning why 3 nulls are not included in the figures
The figures in this chapter draw four substrates: the connectome, the weight-permuted 
control, and two of the nulls, Erdős–Rényi and the degree-preserving
rewire, which is forty cells at each parcellation. The clustering- and
modularity-preserving rewires enter as numbers rather than as panels, in
section~\ref{sec:act1-bulk-gap-placement}, for the reason given there. The
random gaussian rung takes no part in the comparison at all: its weights are
signed, so it is not a non-negative matrix, and the statistic this chapter
reports is defined around a Perron root that a signed matrix does not have.


\section{The spectrum is real, and it has one outlier}
\label{sec:act1-spectrum-real}
\bigskip
% Outline item 3. Carries A1.1. Figure F1a.

% P1: the spectrum is real, and what follows for the figure.
That build makes the substrate symmetric, and symmetry has a consequence for the
spectrum that the whole chapter rests on: a symmetric matrix is normal, so its
eigenvalues are real. This is not an approximation that holds well enough to
work with. Across all forty ladder cells at $N = 448$ the largest imaginary part
in the stored spectra is exactly zero, % A1 2.5
and the symmetry check holds in every row. % A1 2.5
It is also why Figure~\ref{fig:F1} takes the form it does: the eigenvalues are
drawn as a density on the real line rather than as a cloud in the complex plane,
where they would all lie on a single line and the second dimension would carry
nothing.

% P2: one outlier, and the vocabulary the rest of the chapter uses.
What that density looks like on the connectome is panel~(a) of that figure.
Almost all of the mass sits in a dense band about the origin, and one eigenvalue
stands well clear of it at the right-hand edge. The separation is not an
accident of this particular graph, and non-negativity is why. The
Perron--Frobenius theorem guarantees a non-negative matrix a real, positive
leading eigenvalue, largest in modulus, whose eigenvector is non-negative
throughout; the only question a given substrate answers is how far clear of
everything else that eigenvalue sits. Three terms follow from the picture and
are used for the rest of this thesis. The \emph{Perron root} $\lambda_1$ is that
leading eigenvalue. The \emph{bulk} is the band holding the remainder. The
\emph{gap} is the distance between them. All three are properties of a single
substrate, measured before any comparison is made.

% P3: how the bulk edge is measured, and the one thing to know about it.
The bulk needs an edge if it is to be measured, and the one used throughout is
$\mathrm{bulk95}$: the 95th percentile of $|\lambda|$ over the spectrum, divided
by $|\lambda_1|$, so that it is expressed as a fraction of the Perron root
rather than in the units of the weights. % A1 2.6 finding 1
The word outlier then invites one assumption that the definition does not
license. The Perron eigenvalue is inside the population the percentile is taken
over. % A1 2.6 finding 1
It is an outlier in where it sits, not one the statistic sets aside, and the
bulk edge is therefore a percentile of the whole spectrum rather than of the
spectrum with its largest element removed.

\section{The bulk is everyone's, the gap is not, and it is placement}
\label{sec:act1-bulk-gap-placement}
\bigskip
% Outline items 4 and 5, merged per act1_structure.md section 5 item 16.
% Carries A1.2, A1.3, A1.5. Figures F1, F2.

% P1: the decomposition, in raw units. Carries A1.2.
The four spectra differ in one place only, and it is not the bulk.
Figure~\ref{fig:F1} draws the eigenvalues of the un-rescaled matrix $W$, one
substrate per row, on a shared axis in the raw units of the weights, and the
first thing to do with it is to sight down the column. The four bulk bands sit
at the same place: their edges fall between
0.0587 % TIER0 3.1
and 0.0614, % TIER0 3.1
a spread of 4.4\% % TIER0 3.1
of their mean at $N = 448$, % TIER0 2.1
taking a spread throughout as the range over the mean. % A1 5.2
What moves is the outlier. The Perron root runs from
0.1061 % TIER0 3.1
at the Erdős–Rényi rung to 0.1889 % TIER0 3.1
at the connectome, so the grey region separating the bulk from $\lambda_1$ grows
steadily up the column while the coloured band beneath it stays put.
Figure~\ref{fig:F2} makes the same decomposition as medians across the whole
ladder rather than as drawn spectra: the absolute bulk in panel~(a), the Perron
root in panel~(b), and Table~\ref{tab:act1-ladder} gives the full set at both
parcellations. 
Nothing about the bulks has moved between those two numbers. Only the divisor
has.

% P2: what the normalisation manufactures. Carries A1.2, second half.
That divisor is what panel~(e) makes visible. It redraws the same spectra as
cumulative distributions after each substrate has been divided by its own
$|\lambda_1|$, 
which is the conventional normalisation approach every spectral-radius-matched
comparison in connectome-reservoir literature uses 
\cite{suarez2021learning, suarez2024connectome, milisav2026neuromorphic}. 
In these units the curves separate everywhere and the connectome's reads as the compressed one. 
The crossings of the 95\% level are $\mathrm{bulk95}$ itself and can be read 
off the axis:
0.325 % A1 3, F1 caption
for the connectome against 0.52 to 0.55 % A1 3, F1 caption
for the nulls. The two halves of the figure are the same spectra one division
apart. Dividing by the single quantity on which these substrates differ turns a
common bulk and a separated outlier into a separated bulk, and that is the whole
of the difference between the two panels.

% P3: the gap ratio and the identity. Carries A1.3.
The statistic worth reporting is therefore the ratio of the two, $|\lambda_1|$
over the absolute bulk radius, which panel~(f) draws per seed. It is
3.078 % TIER0 3.1
for the connectome against 1.81 to 1.92 % TIER0 3.1
for the three nulls, so the connectome stands roughly
$1.7\times$ % TIER0 3.1
clear of every one of them. This is not a further measurement. The gap ratio,
the inverse bulk and the critical scale are the same number,
$|\lambda_1| / \mathrm{abs\_bulk} = 1/\mathrm{bulk95} = \mathrm{sr\_crit}$, % TIER0 3.1
which is why panel~(f) can carry $\mathrm{bulk95}$ on its right-hand axis, and
why the name $\mathrm{sr\_crit}$ costs nothing to adopt: it names the quantity
after what it measures, the nominal spectral radius at which the median seed's
bulk edge reaches one.\footnote{The edge in question is the 95th percentile of
$|\lambda|$, % A1 2.6 finding 1
so at $\sigma = \mathrm{sr\_crit}$ five per cent % A1 2.6 finding 1
of modes already exceed unity. $\mathrm{sr\_crit}$ is a criticality scale each
substrate carries with it, not the point at which that substrate becomes
supercritical. The identity above holds under the product-of-medians
aggregation used throughout; taking the median of the per-seed products instead
breaks it by up to 0.055. % A1 2.3
}

% P4: the retraction, stated once, in the direction the data supports.
It is worth saying plainly what this replaces. An earlier reading of these same
spectra, and the one the connectome-reservoir literature invites, is that
biological weight placement produces an anomalously compact bulk. It does not.
The bulk is essentially everyone's, and what placement raises is the Perron
root, by a factor of 1.78 % TIER0 3.1
over the Erdős–Rényi rung at $N = 448$. % TIER0 3.1(a)
The compactness is an artefact of reporting the spectrum in units of
$|\lambda_1|$, which is to say in units of the one quantity that differs. The
finding is the same fact stated in the direction the data supports: an
anomalously large spectral gap.

% P5: it is placement, and what that does not license. Carries A1.5.
What produces that gap is answered by the second row of the figure. The
weight-permuted control differs from the connectome in nothing but which edge
carries which weight, and it lands with the nulls, at a gap ratio of
1.922 % TIER0 3.1
against the connectome's 3.078. % TIER0 3.1
Neither the topology nor the weight distribution is sufficient on its own, and
the alignment between the two is what buys the gap. That conclusion has a limit,
and leaving it to be inferred would overstate it. The contrast localises the
effect to placement; it does not say which feature of the placement is
responsible, and no mechanism for why placement produces the gap is claimed here
or anywhere in this thesis.

% P6: the wider ladder, as corroboration of A1.5.
The control settles that; the wider ladder shows it by degrees. Two further
rungs hold more of the connectome's topology fixed than the degree-preserving
rewire does, one preserving its community structure and one its clustering, and
neither recovers the gap. Their gap ratios are 1.95 % TIER0 3.1(b)
and 2.08 % TIER0 3.1(b)
at $N = 448$, against the connectome's 3.078, and at the larger parcellation
they fall inside the same band as the two nulls the figures draw. Taking all
six non-negative substrates together, the smallest margin anywhere is
1.48-fold % TIER0 3.1(b), derived
at $N = 448$ and 1.63-fold % TIER0 3.1(b), derived
at $N = 1000$. Preserving the degree sequence, then the community structure,
then the clustering coefficient recovers none of what placement supplies, so the
gap is not a property of the connectome's degree distribution or of its
modular organisation, and holding progressively more of its topology fixed does
not approach it. These rungs corroborate the claim; the claim itself rests on
the control, which holds the topology exactly rather than one statistic of it
at a time.

\section{The gap survives a change of scale}
\label{sec:act1-scale}
\bigskip
% Outline item 6. Carries A1.4. Figures F2 (both scales), S1 (appendix).

% P1: what carries, the separation.
Every result so far has been a statement about one parcellation. The whole
ladder was rebuilt at $N = 1000$, % TIER0 2.1
a 2.2-fold % TIER0 1.4
change in the number of nodes, and the question this section asks is which of
the chapter's findings survive it. Two parcellations are one step and there is
no third, so what follows is a robustness check across a single change of scale
rather than a demonstration of invariance. The separation survives it, which is
what panel~(c) of Figure~\ref{fig:F2} shows: at the
against 2.30 to 2.44 % TIER0 3.1
for the three nulls, which is the same clearance it had at the smaller one. That
is the sense in which the gap, and only the gap, is the scale-robust finding of
this chapter.

% P2: what loosens, the near-identity of the bulks.
The near-identity of the bulks does not survive in the same way, and the two
must be kept apart in every sentence that mentions either. In panel~(a) of the
same figure the four bars at the two scales tell different stories: at
$N = 448$ the four absolute bulk radii agree to 4.4\% % TIER0 3.1
of their mean; at $N = 1000$ they spread 6.4\%, % TIER0 3.1
or 6.9\% % TIER0 3.1
if the per-seed products are taken before the median rather than after, so at
this scale the aggregation has to be quoted with the number. The loosening is
also narrower than that summary suggests: three of the four substrates still sit
within 1.1\% % TIER0 3.1(a)
of one another, and the whole of the difference among the four is the is the Erdős–Rényi rung sitting
low. \emph{Merely close} is the right description of the set at the larger
parcellation and \emph{near-identical} is a description of $N = 448$, which is
why the agreement of the bulks and the robustness of the separation are never
carried by one sentence.

% P3: what reverses, the ordering among the nulls.
The third thing scale does is reverse an ordering. Ranked by $\mathrm{bulk95}$,
the three nulls do not fall in the same order at the two parcellations, and the
same instability appears in the absolute bulk, whose largest value belongs to
the connectome at $N = 448$ and to the degree-preserving rewire at
$N = 1000$. % TIER0 3.1(a)
Nothing in this thesis rests on either ordering, and the reason to record the
reversal is to justify a discipline the chapter has been observing throughout:
an ordering among the nulls is quoted at a named scale, or it is not quoted.
Figure~\ref{fig:S1} rebuilds Figure~\ref{fig:F1} at the larger parcellation and
is where the consequence is visible. Its rows are in ladder order, as F1's are,
and at $N = 1000$ their gap ratios are consequently not monotone down the
column. What the figure shows is the connectome's separation from all three
nulls; the arrangement among them is not to be read off it.



\section{What carries forward: a critical scale and a mode}
\label{sec:act1-handover}
\bigskip
% Outline item 7. No new claims. One paragraph. No numerals by design.

Two things leave this chapter and are used in the next. The first is
$\mathrm{sr\_crit}$; the nominal spectral radius at which the median
seed’s bulk edge reaches one. Because the gap ratio is also the inverse bulk, every
substrate arrives carrying its own critical scale, the spectral radius at which
its bulk edge reaches one, and the four substrates do not carry the same one.
That is what makes a second matching axis possible at all: alongside the nominal
spectral radius, which holds the Perron root fixed, the product
$\sigma \cdot \mathrm{bulk95}$ holds the bulk fixed instead. What this chapter
supplies is the quantity that second axis is built from, and no more than that.
It does not make the axis neutral, and for the reason
Chapter~\ref{chapter:methods} gave: the substrates differ in one quantity only,
so each axis fixes exactly what the other lets vary. The second thing handed on
is the Perron mode itself. This chapter has established that it stands clear of
the bulk, and that weight placement is what puts it there; it has established
nothing at all about what the mode does. The spectrum is a description of the
operator, and the question the next chapter asks is where each part of it ends
up in the activity that operator generates. The mode arrives there as an object
to be decomposed rather than as an explanation, and it arrives with the limit
set out above still attached to it.
