\label{sec:act2-handover}

% P1: what arrives, and the question. No results.
Two things arrive from Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-I}, and neither is a statement
about activity. The first is $\mathrm{sr\_crit}$, the operating point at which a
substrate's own bulk edge reaches one:
3.078 % TIER0 2.1
for the connectome against
1.81 to 1.92 % TIER0 2.1
for the three nulls, a scale each substrate carries with it rather than one the
comparison imposes. The second is the Perron mode. The previous chapter
established that it stands clear of the bulk and that weight placement is what
puts it there, and it arrives here as an object to be decomposed rather than as
an explanation. A readout sees neither. What it sees is the activity the network
produces when it is driven, and the question of this chapter is which part of the
connectivity's eigenspectrum ends up where in that activity.

% P2: the object, the identity, and the pointer to the schematic.
Drive the reservoir, record every unit at every timestep and discard a warmup,
and the result is the state matrix $A$: on the memory-capacity task,
2500 rows by 448 columns. % A2 2.5
A linear readout does not use $A$ directly. It inverts the \textbf{Gram matrix}
$G = A^{\top}A$, whose eigenvalues say how much of the trajectory lies along each
direction the activity explores, so $G$ is two things at once, a description of
the manifold's shape and the budget a linear readout has to spend on it. It
splits exactly:

\bigskip
\begin{equation}
    \underbrace{A^{\top}A \bracegap}_{\text{Gram matrix}}
    \;=\;
    \underbrace{\tilde{A}^{\top}\tilde{A} \bracegap}_{\substack{\text{fluctuations} \\ \text{about the mean}}}
    \;+\;
    \underbrace{T_{\mathrm{eff}}\,m\,m^{\top} \bracegap}_{\substack{\text{rank one:} \\ \text{the time-average}}}
    \label{eq:gram-split}
\end{equation}

where $m$ holds each unit's mean over time and $\tilde{A}$ the time-centred
states. The second term is built from one number per unit, the level that unit
holds on average, so it has rank one: a single fixed pattern the network carries
throughout the run. The first is everything that changes from timestep to
timestep, and therefore the only part that can carry information about a changing
input. Figure~\ref{fig:F20} draws the construction. The identity settles nothing
on its own. It says the time-average lives in a rank-one term, not what that term
consists of, and that is a measurement rather than an algebraic fact.

% P3: the two-spectra convention. Governs every later sentence.
Two matrices have eigenvalues in this chapter and they are not both called a
spectrum. The eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ of the connectivity $W$ are Chapter
\ref{chapter:act-I}'s object, and where they are meant the text says so. The
eigenvalues $g_i$ of the Gram matrix are properties of the states a network
produces when it is driven at a given operating point by a given input, not
properties of $W$, and where they are meant the text says \emph{Gram spectrum} in
full. Nothing below obtains the second from the first.

% P4: criticality, fixed before the word is used.
One word needs fixing before it is used. Criticality, in the usual sense, is the
point at which the leading eigenvalue of the scaled recurrent matrix reaches one,
which here is $\sigma = 1$ exactly, since scaling to a nominal radius sets
$|\lambda_1| = \sigma$. $\mathrm{sr\_crit}$ is not that point and is not a
critical point at all. It is where a substrate's bulk edge reaches one, so at
$\sigma = \mathrm{sr\_crit}$ the leading mode has been expansive for a long while
and five per cent % A1 2.6 finding 1
of modes have only just joined it. \emph{Supercritical} below means an operating
point of at least 3.05, % TIER0 3.2
the connectome's own $\mathrm{sr\_crit}$ rounded to the measurement grid and
applied to every substrate, which is the conservative choice for reasons
Section~\ref{sec:act2-counting} gives. Both labels describe the linear operator.
What the units are doing at a given operating point also depends on where the
nonlinearity has put them, and no claim below rests on the labels.
Where the chapter measures follows from
what it is for. The capacity result it supplies a mechanism for is a
supercritical one, since at the tuned optimum the substrates are at parity, so
the geometry is measured where the difference to be explained exists rather than
where a benchmark would look for it.

% P5: scope, stated up front rather than in a limitations section.
Three limits bind everything that follows. Every result is measured at
$N = 448$, % TIER0 2.1
on empirical non-negative weights, and on the memory-capacity task, whose input
is white noise, % A2 1
except where the text states otherwise. Nothing in this chapter alters the
weights themselves; that intervention belongs to
Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-III}. The comparison of
structural bases was never recorded across the null ladder, so nothing drawn
from it is a statement about the substrates. % TIER0 3.12
And the common-mode amplitude carries an arbitrary sign, settled by the input a
given run happens to draw, so each run's value is made positive before runs are
combined, never after; Chapter~\ref{chapter:methods} gives what the wrong order
costs.

% P6: d_eff defined. Justification deferred, deliberately.
That leaves the measure. These Gram spectra fall smoothly across ten orders of
magnitude without reaching zero, so every substrate has full numerical rank and
any cutoff placed by hand would decide the answer before measuring it. The
readout already implies a rule. Ridge regression shrinks each direction by a
factor set by how far its Gram eigenvalue stands above the regularisation
strength $\alpha$, and adding those factors gives the \textbf{ridge effective
rank},
\begin{equation}
    d_{\mathrm{eff}}(\alpha)
    \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{g_i}{g_i + \alpha},
    \label{eq:deff}
\end{equation}
where $g_1 \ge \dots \ge g_N$ are the eigenvalues of $G$. Each direction
contributes close to one when its eigenvalue stands well above $\alpha$, close to
zero when it lies well below, and exactly one half at the crossover, so the sum
counts how many directions survive the regularisation with a soft boundary rather
than a sharp one. The subscript is for \emph{effective}: $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is
the dimensionality of the state space as this readout can use it, and identically
the effective degrees of freedom of the fitted
readout.\footnote{The term \emph{effective rank} is also used for a quantity
computed on the recurrent matrix itself~\cite{clark2025connectivity}, which is a
different object from the one measured here. Chapter~\ref{chapter:methods}
separates the two.}
This is why $\alpha$ is called a \emph{floor} throughout: it is the level in the
Gram spectrum below which a direction stops being worth anything to the readout.
It is not a parameter of the measure. It is the readout's own value, the same
number the memory task is fitted with. Why this count rather than a
variance-weighted one is a result and not a definition, and
Section~\ref{sec:act2-counting} makes that case; making it here would leave the
measure looking assumed.

\label{sec:act2-perron}

% P1: A2.1. The measurement, first sentence. Aggregation in the footnote.
Once the states are time-centred, the network's own leading dynamical modes are
almost nowhere to be found. On the connectome driven at its own $\mathrm{sr\_crit}$ 
($\sigma = 3.05$), the top eigenvector of $W$ accounts for
0.0006 % A2 2.1
of the fluctuation variance where a direction drawn at random accounts for
0.0025, % A2 2.1
and it stays below chance in all ten seeds at every $k$ up to
five. % A2 F4
In Figure~\ref{fig:F4}(a) the curve climbs back to the random baseline only
around $k = 20$, % A2 F4
and tracks it from there on.\footnote{Those are the memory-task values, which

0.0001 % TIER0 3.12

29 of the 30 % A2 2.1

% P2: the control that makes P1 informative. Harmonics introduced and
% motivated. Not a claim about where the manifold lives.
Taken by itself, that admits a duller reading, in which no structural basis
lines up with these fluctuations at all and the measurement is uninformative
rather than the modes unusual. A second basis settles it, and one with a claim
on this substrate in particular. The eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian are the
standing wave patterns a network's wiring supports, ordered from the smoothest,
in which neighbouring parcels move together, to the most rapidly alternating;
they are the network's analogue of Fourier modes, and on a connectome they carry
the further name of \emph{connectome harmonics}, having been shown to organise measured
cortical activity in humans~\cite{atasoy2016human}. If any basis read off this
substrate's structure ought to align with the activity it produces, in other
words, it is this one. It does: measured on the same states in the same panel,
the leading harmonics sit
7.6 times % A2 F4
\emph{above} chance at $k = 1$. Some structural bases do align with these
fluctuations, and the dominant modes the Perron root belongs to are precisely
the ones that do not. Which basis leads turns out to be a consequence of the
weights being non-negative rather than a fact about the connectome, which
Appendix~\ref{app:sign-basis} takes up. Both halves of that comparison are
supercritical, and only there does it decide anything: at the one near-critical
point this capture also holds, the harmonics stand at chance on the
memory-capacity task, so no basis separates from the baseline and the duller
reading cannot be ruled out.\footnote{At
$\sigma = 0.947$ % A2 audit 23
the memory-task medians put the harmonics at
1.03 times chance % A2 audit 23
and the dominant modes at roughly a fifth of it, in
7 of 10 seeds; % A2 audit 23
pooled across the three tasks the same point reverses outright, the dominant
modes standing 4.2 times % A2 audit 23
above chance, and the reversal is carried by the chaotic task alone
(16.8 times % A2 audit 23
its own baseline, in all ten seeds). Two operating points cannot locate where
that changes, and nothing here infers a transition between them.}

% P3: they went into the rank-one term, and how much it holds is not obvious.
Perron--Frobenius accounts for the absence of the network's own leading dynamical modes. 
The leading eigenvector of a non-negative matrix is positive throughout, so the mode it belongs to drives
every unit in the same direction at once, which is a fixed pattern rather than a
fluctuation, and a fixed pattern is precisely what the rank-one term of
Equation~\ref{eq:gram-split} holds. What the identity cannot say is how much of the activity that place
consumes. On the cell drawn in Figure~\ref{fig:F20} the
rank-one term holds 51.3\% % A2 2.7
of the trace, which is to say that half of the matrix the readout inverts is a
single fixed pattern carrying nothing about the input. Driving a network harder
can only raise that share, since units pushed into the flat regions of their
nonlinearity move less and less about wherever they have settled. 
How far it grows, and whether some substrates give it more than others, the
identity cannot settle.

% P4: A2.2 and the inversion, which is the finding.
Panel~(b) of Figure~\ref{fig:F4} follows the common-mode amplitude across the
sweep for all four substrates, and all four rise as they are driven harder. The
nulls rise first, as their spectra require, since their bulk edges reach one
between 1.81 and 1.92 % TIER0 2.1
where the connectome's holds out until 3.078. % TIER0 3.1
What matters is where the curves end up. At $\sigma = 6$ the connectome sits at
0.759 % TIER0 3.7
against the nulls' 0.949 to 0.989, % TIER0 3.7
and at $\sigma = 2$ the separation is wider still,
0.114 % TIER0 3.7
against 0.532 to 0.593. % TIER0 3.7
The least common-mode dominated substrate on this ladder is the one carrying by
far the largest Perron root. That inversion is the finding of this section, and
it is not a paradox once the gap rather than the root is taken as the operative
quantity: what distinguishes the connectome is the distance between its leading
eigenvalue and its bulk, and a separation of that width lets the leading mode be
driven hard while the bulk stays where it is. The nulls have no such latitude.
Their bulk sits close behind their Perron root, so raising the gain carries the
whole spectrum up together, the units saturate in step, and an amplitude
approaching one is what that looks like from outside.

% P5: what follows, what does not, and the question for section 3.
The decomposition is therefore clean, and the rest of this chapter can work
inside the fluctuation term and treat the rank-one term as accounted for. It is
also now established that the substrates differ in how much of their activity
that term consumes, in the opposite direction to the one their connectivity
spectra suggest. What none of this establishes is anything about capacity, since
being least dominated by the common mode is not the same as having the most
directions a readout can act on, and nothing measured here touches how the
surviving fluctuations are \emph{arranged} across directions rather than how much
of them is left. Arrangement is what a readout is finally sensitive to, through
the floor of Equation~\ref{eq:deff}, and it is where the next section goes.

% P3: the rate, with the both-ends warning taken off the algebra first.
Where a Gram spectrum sits and how many directions it would lose if the floor
rose are different questions, and the second is easier to get backwards.
Differentiating Equation~\ref{eq:deff} with respect to the floor gives the
answer directly,
\begin{equation}
    -\frac{\mathrm{d}\,d_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\mathrm{d}\log\alpha}
    \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{g_i\,\alpha}{(g_i + \alpha)^{2}} ,
    \label{eq:floor-sensitivity}
\end{equation}
the directions lost per $e$-fold rise in $\alpha$, which converts to a loss per
decade by a factor of $\ln 10$ and is reported per decade throughout. Each
direction contributes most to that sum when it sits exactly \emph{at} the floor,
and less on either side of it. A small sum therefore has two quite different
causes, a spectrum standing well clear of the floor and a spectrum that has
already dropped beneath it, and a low sensitivity read on its own cannot tell
them apart. Erdős--Rényi is the second case. Reading its low
absolute figure as robustness would invert the truth: per unit of surviving
dimensionality it is the \emph{most} floor-sensitive substrate on this ladder,
shedding 29.1\% % TIER0 3.6(c)
of its effective rank per decade of $\alpha$ against the connectome's
5.6\%. % TIER0 3.6(c)
The two lose almost the same number of directions in absolute terms. One has
some four hundred to lose them from and the other has seventy-five.

% P4: A2.7 first half, the interior minima. Radius-dependence is the point.
Neither the position nor the rate is a fixed property of a substrate, since both
depend on how hard it is being driven. Panel~(b) of Figure~\ref{fig:F18} draws
Equation~\ref{eq:floor-sensitivity} across the sweep, and the curve has two
humps. It vanishes at zero radius, where a network barely driven has almost no
spectrum above the floor to lose, rises as directions are pushed up through the
floor, and falls again once they stand clear of it. The minima that matter are
therefore the \emph{interior} dips between the two humps, and they fall at
different radii for different substrates: the connectome's near
3.58, % TIER0 3.6(b)
every null's between 1.58 and 2.00, % TIER0 3.6(b)
a separation that holds seed by seed rather than emerging from the
medians.\footnote{The qualifier \emph{interior} is not droppable. The
connectome's lowest sensitivity anywhere over non-zero radius lies not at its
dip but far out on the weakly driven limb, % TIER0 3.6(b)
where the reservoir has almost nothing left to lose; read as a global minimum,
this half of the result does not reproduce.}

% P5: A2.7 second half, the migration, compressed. Consistent-with, not fitted.
That dependence leaves something to check. Raising the regularisation penalises
operating points at which many directions sit near the floor, so each
substrate's best operating point should move toward its own interior dip, and
panel~(c) is where it does: across a grid of $\alpha$ spanning five orders of
magnitude % TIER0 3.6(d)
the connectome's ridge-optimal radius moves from
2.4 to 3.6 % TIER0 3.6
and every null moves once, from
1.2 to 1.6. % TIER0 3.6
Direction and endpoint are all that is claimed. Nothing is fitted, and the dips
in panel~(b) and the optima in panel~(c) are measured on different radius grids,
so the connectome's 3.58 % TIER0 3.6(b)
and its 3.6 % TIER0 3.6
are two grids' nearest points to one place rather than one number recovered

% P6: what the section amounts to, and the handover into section 4.
Each substrate, then, has an operating point at which its Gram spectrum stands
clear of the readout floor, and what separates them is where that point falls
rather than whether they have one. The connectome's lies higher, so it is still
near its own at radii where the nulls are long past theirs, which is a claim
about the range of operating points over which a substrate stays usable rather
than about how good any of them is at its best. One consequence of these
positions is worth carrying into the next section. The floor lies some ten
orders of magnitude % A2 4.5.3
below the leading variance, so a substrate can have hundreds of directions
standing clear of it that carry almost none of the variance between them.
Whether those directions are counted at all depends on how a dimensionality
measure weights them, and the measure the field reaches for first weights by
variance.






\section{Which counting scheme sees it}
\label{sec:act2-counting}
\bigskip
% Outline item 5 (act2_manifold.md section 4). Carries A2.4 and A2.5, together
% contribution 6. Figure F6 (a, b, c). Chain step 4 in CROSS_ACT_SPINE.md.
% Sources: fact sheet 10. The participation ratio's definition, Equation 3.x

% FORBIDDEN here (sheet 10): any sentence carrying one number from the

% P1: the discovery, in the order it happened. PR first because it was first.
Dimensionality of neural population activity is usually counted with the participation
ratio \cite{gao2017theory}, which weights each direction of the state covariance by its share of the
variance, and it is the measure this work reached for first. Across the seven
substrates of the wider null ladder that count moves
16\%, % TIER0 3.12
from 1.19 to 1.38: % TIER0 3.12
seven networks whose activity occupies essentially the same number of
dimensions, on the ordinary reading. Measured memory capacity over those same
substrates moves roughly three-fold. % A2 2.2
The standard instrument shows nothing where the substrates plainly differ.

% P2: the same states, the other count, and the size of the disagreement.
The ridge effective rank of those same recorded states moves
5.5-fold, % TIER0 3.12
from 75 to 413 % TIER0 3.12
of the 448 directions available. These are not two experiments. Both counts are
computed from the same recorded states, on the same cells, one before centring
and one after, and one of them reports substrates that are barely
distinguishable while the other reports substrates that differ enormously.
Figure~\ref{fig:F6} is that disagreement, and panel~(a) is where its cause
becomes visible.

% P3: both are weighted counts; the mechanism on one cell.
The two measures are more alike than the disagreement suggests, which is what
makes the disagreement informative rather than merely awkward. Each is exactly a
sum of one weight per direction. For the ridge effective rank that is
Equation~\ref{eq:deff} as written, every direction weighted by
$g_i/(g_i + \alpha)$, how far its Gram eigenvalue clears the floor. For the
participation ratio it is true but less apparent: writing
$p_i = \mu_i / \sum_j \mu_j$ for direction $i$'s share of the variance, the
definition rearranges to $\sum_i p_i / \sum_j p_j^{2}$, each direction weighted
by that share. Both are counts, so each curve's area in panel~(a) is the number
it is labelled with, and what the panel shows is how differently the two weight
the same directions. On one connectome cell the ridge weight stands above one
half for 438 of the 448 % A2 2.4
directions. The variance weight has collapsed by the fifth, two directions
carrying 95\% % A2 2.4
of a participation ratio of 1.28. % A2 2.4

% P4: why the variance-weighted count misses it. Consequence of section 3.
Panel~(a) is a consequence of the previous section rather than a second finding.
The floor lies some ten orders of magnitude % A2 4.5.3
below the leading variance, so a direction can stand far clear of the floor,
fully available to the readout, while holding a share of the variance too small
to register in a weighted count. Hundreds of the connectome's directions are in
exactly that position, and Section~\ref{sec:act2-floor} is where they were
counted. The two measures have not disagreed about the data. The quantity one of
them weights by and the quantity that decides availability have come apart, and
they come apart precisely where the substrates were found to differ.

% P5: the test against measured capacity. One aggregation unit per sentence.
Which of the two is registering something real is a question the data can settle,
by asking which count orders what the readout actually achieves. Memory capacity
enters here as the quantity a dimensionality measure is judged against rather
than as a performance claim: what is being ordered is the counting schemes, not
the substrates. Taking one median per substrate across the seven, the ridge
effective rank orders them against measured capacity at a rank correlation of
+1.000 % TIER0 3.12
and the participation ratio at +0.107. % TIER0 3.12
Pooled instead over all 350 % TIER0 3.12
individual cells within the supercritical regime, the pair reads
+0.998 % TIER0 3.12
against +0.308. % TIER0 3.12
Those are two tests on two aggregation units, and a number from one may not be
set against a number from the other, though they agree on everything that
matters. Correlating either measure against position in the null ladder is a
different quantity again, weak and negative for both, and it is not the claim.

% P6: the ceiling, which bounds the ordering.
One limit belongs with that, since it bounds what the ordering can mean. Every
substrate's effective rank peaks within a few per cent of the hard ceiling at
$N$, % TIER0 3.2
so peak dimensionality is unresolvable at this parcellation and the ordering
above lives in the decay region beyond the peak rather than at the peak itself.

% P7: what the discovery amounts to, and its scope.
None of this makes the participation ratio the wrong measure. It counts what it
claims to count, how concentrated the variance of the states is, and for the
question a neural-manifold analysis usually asks it remains the right
instrument. The claim is narrower and, perhaps, more interesting: that quantity
is not the one a ridge readout can act on, so a measure of dimensionality is
meaningful only relative to what is reading the system out. Two counts of the
same states can disagree this sharply without either being in error, and
choosing between them is a question about the observer rather than about the
data. The demonstration is empirical and its scope should be read narrowly: one
task, one family of substrates, one readout. That total capacity is bounded by
the number of linearly independent state variables is established, and this is a
case of that bound rather than a rival to
it~\cite{dambre2012information}.




\section{The axis that does not move}
\label{sec:act2-temporal}
\bigskip
% Additional section, not in the act2_manifold.md seven-item outline. Carries
% no claim from the register; reports a negative result that bounds the
% chapter. Placed here per CONVENTIONS working rule 8: negative results go
% where the claim they bound is made. No figure: the curvature figure belongs
% to Act III. Sources: fact sheet 12; A2 section 5 item 15; TIER0 3.11.
% FORBIDDEN (sheet 12): bimodality as task-independent or as a property of the
% manifold; any graded curvature or trajectory-straightness account; "the
% manifold has been fully characterised"; "curvature is flat, so geometry does
% not matter"; "sigma_eff crosses 1"; "the connectome is a better reservoir";
% "compact bulk".

% P1: the second axis, measured and flat.
A trajectory has a shape as well as a spread. Everything above concerns the
spread: how many directions the activity occupies, and how many of them a
readout can act on. Shape is a separate property, and the natural way to measure
it is the angle between successive steps along the path, averaged over the
trajectory. A path running nearly straight turns by a little; one reversing on
itself turns by close to $\pi$. Because the measure is computed on displacements
rather than positions, the common mode's contribution to the time-average
cancels in the difference, so it reports shape and not offset.

% P2: it does not move, while capacity does. Ranges named.
That quantity was recorded alongside everything else in this chapter, on all
three tasks, and it is reported here because it does not move. On the chaotic
prediction task the seed-median curvature is
0.261 % A2 5.15
radians for the connectome and
0.263 % A2 5.15
for Erdős--Rényi across the whole sweep in $\sigma$, % A2 5.15
and it is flat between those endpoints rather than merely equal at them. Over
the same range the network's predictive horizon falls by roughly
a factor of ten. % TIER0 3.11
The two noise-driven tasks sit at much higher curvature and are equally flat.
That the levels differ between tasks is a fact about the inputs rather than
about the networks: a chaotic attractor is a smooth low-dimensional signal, so
successive steps of the trajectory point in similar directions, whereas a
white-noise drive delivers an independent kick at every timestep and successive
displacements are close to uncorrelated. What the substrate contributes, on all
three, is nothing.

% P3: what that does and does not license.
Two readings are available and only one is supported. It would be wrong to
conclude that the geometry of the trajectory does not matter, since a quantity
that does not vary can neither explain anything nor be ruled out. What the
measurement establishes is narrower and more awkward: on the non-negative
weights this chapter is measured on, prediction degrades sharply while the shape
of the trajectory stays put, so whatever governs that degradation is not visible
on this axis, and the dimensional account developed above does not reach it
either. What does govern it is left open here and named as open. This is a limit
on the chapter rather than a detail of it. The activity manifold has two axes,
and what precedes this section is an account of one.\footnote{One qualifier
travels with any later statement about trajectory shape. Curvature on the
chaotic task is sharply bimodal, with under one per cent of cells falling
between the two clusters, but that is a property of the manifold \emph{and the
signal driving it}: on the two noise-driven tasks roughly half of all cells sit
in the band the chaotic task leaves empty. % A2 5.15
The bimodality is real, and it is not general.}




\section{From description to intervention}
\label{sec:act2-toact3}
\bigskip
% Outline item 7 (act2_manifold.md section 4). Carries no new claim and points
% at no figure. Hands over four things, the fourth a boundary rather than a
% result. Sources: fact sheet 12. Does NOT restate the chain and does NOT
% re-argue Section 5.5's boundary.
% FORBIDDEN here (sheet 12): bimodality stated as task-independent; any graded
% curvature or trajectory-straightness account; "the manifold has been fully
% characterised"; "curvature is flat, so geometry does not matter";
% "sigma_eff crosses 1 at the transition"; "the connectome is a better
% reservoir"; "the manifold lives in low-frequency graph harmonics";
% "compact bulk".

% P1: everything so far is correlational. The intervention, introduced here.
Everything measured in this chapter is a description. The connectome is less
dominated by its common mode than its nulls are, its Gram spectrum stands
further clear of the readout floor, and it has more usable directions as a
consequence; but these were observed together, not shown to depend on one
another. If the common mode is what governs the rest, removing it should remove
the difference. That test is available because the common mode exists for a
reason: Perron--Frobenius guarantees it only while the weights are non-negative,
so making a fraction of them negative dissolves the guarantee and leaves the
topology untouched. Chapter~\ref{chapter:act-III} grades that fraction from zero
upward and asks whether the dimensional advantage goes with the mechanism
proposed for it. Where this chapter compared weight families side by side, the
next intervenes on the connectome itself.

% P2: radius-dependence is what lets the same axis be read twice.
The second thing carried forward is why that test has to run across operating
points rather than at one. Section~\ref{sec:act2-floor} found that where a
substrate's Gram spectrum sits relative to the floor changes with how hard the
network is driven, so how many directions a readout can use is a property of a
substrate at an operating point rather than of the substrate. That is what
allows the same axis to be read twice in the next chapter: once for how much of
a past input a network can retain, and once for how far into the future it can
carry a signal of its own. The ridge effective rank goes forward as the
instrument for the first of those, earned in
Section~\ref{sec:act2-counting} rather than assumed, and with its scope
attached.

% P3: the boundary, named not re-argued, and what it means for the next chapter.
The third is the boundary of Section~\ref{sec:act2-temporal}. It goes forward
unresolved, and it does not stay flat: the axis that does not move at
$f = 0$ begins to move once negative weights enter. So the intervention runs on
both halves of the problem at once, and what it finds on the second is not a
continuation of what this chapter has measured but a different regime, arriving
where the geometry starts to change.
